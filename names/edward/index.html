Edward in Korean 에드워드

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Edward as 에드워드: four syllables, said e-deu-wo-deu. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Pronunciation tip

드 and 드 carry the filler vowel ㅡ (eu). Keep them short and light, almost just the consonant.

  • ㅝ in 워 is one gliding vowel: wuh, in a single beat.
  • ㅇ at the top of 워 is silent, so 워 is just the vowel: wo.
  • Give all 4 syllables the same weight. Korean has no strong stress, so it's e-deu-wo-deu, evenly.

Korean given names are almost always two syllables (after a one-syllable family name), so at 4 syllables 에드워드 reads as a foreign name at a glance. With foreign names some Koreans skip 야 and just say 에드워드 or 에드워드 씨.

Why it's spelled 에드워드

Spelled letter-by-letter you'd get 에드와드, but Korean media write 에드워드, because the name is written the way it sounds, not the way it's spelled.

  • An r after a vowel (before a consonant or at the end) isn't written. Korean spelling follows British-style pronunciation, where that r is silent.
  • w merges with the vowel after it into one Korean vowel: 워.
  • Korean adds the vowel ㅡ to consonants that have no vowel of their own (드 and 드), which is why Edward has 4 syllables in Korean.

Questions people ask

How do you write Edward in Korean?

Edward is written 에드워드 in Hangul (Revised Romanization: edeuwodeu). It is the standard spelling Korean media use for the name. In Hangul it has four syllables: 에 + 드 + 워 + 드.

How do you pronounce Edward in Korean?

Say it e-deu-wo-deu, with even weight on every syllable. 드 and 드 carry the filler vowel ㅡ (eu). Keep them short and light, almost just the consonant.

Mary in Korean 메리

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Mary as 메리: two syllables, said me-ri. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Mary letter by letter

Each Hangul block is one syllable, built from a consonant, a vowel and sometimes a final consonant underneath.

Pronunciation tip

ㄹ between two vowels (메리) is one quick tap of the tongue, like the tt in American "butter": closer to a soft d than an English r.

  • Two even beats, me-ri: Korean doesn't stress one syllable over the other the way English does.

How Koreans would call you

Friends calling you over
메리야!
메리 ends in a vowel, so it takes 야. Casual, for close friends.
At work or with new people
메리 씨
메리 ssi. Polite, roughly "Mr" or "Ms", but used with the first name.
Customer, client, online
메리 님
메리 nim. What you'll see on a delivery box or a booking.

Korean given names are almost always two syllables, so 메리 already sounds natural to Korean ears. With foreign names some Koreans skip 야 and just say 메리 or 메리 씨.

Questions people ask

How do you write Mary in Korean?

Mary is written 메리 in Hangul (Revised Romanization: meri). It is the standard spelling Korean media use for the name. In Hangul it has two syllables: 메 + 리.

How do you pronounce Mary in Korean?

Say it me-ri, with even weight on every syllable. ㄹ between two vowels (메리) is one quick tap of the tongue, like the tt in American "butter": closer to a soft d than an English r.

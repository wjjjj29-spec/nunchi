Gerald in Korean 제럴드

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Gerald as 제럴드: three syllables, said je-reol-deu. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Pronunciation tip

ㄹ between two vowels (제럴) is one quick tap of the tongue, like the tt in American "butter": closer to a soft d than an English r.

  • 드 carries the filler vowel ㅡ (eu). Keep it short and light, almost just the consonant.
  • ㄹ at the bottom of 럴 is an l: the tongue touches just behind the teeth and stays there.
  • ㅓ (eo) in 럴 is the open "uh" of "sun", not an "o".
  • ㅈ at the start is soft and unvoiced, halfway between ch and j.

제럴드 ssi. Polite, roughly "Mr" or "Ms", but used with the first name.
Customer, client, online
제럴드 님
제럴드 nim. What you'll see on a delivery box or a booking.

Korean given names are almost always two syllables (after a one-syllable family name), so at 3 syllables 제럴드 reads as a foreign name at a glance. With foreign names some Koreans skip 야 and just say 제럴드 or 제럴드 씨.

Spelled letter-by-letter you'd get 제랠드, but Korean media write 제럴드, because the name is written the way it sounds, not the way it's spelled.

  • The unstressed "uh" vowel is written ㅓ (eo), whatever letter English uses for it.
  • Korean adds the vowel ㅡ to consonants that have no vowel of their own (드), which is why Gerald has 3 syllables in Korean.

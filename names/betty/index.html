Betty in Korean 베티

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Betty as 베티: two syllables, said be-ti. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Betty letter by letter

Each Hangul block is one syllable, built from a consonant, a vowel and sometimes a final consonant underneath.

Pronunciation tip

ㅂ at the start is soft and unvoiced, halfway between p and b.

  • Two even beats, be-ti: Korean doesn't stress one syllable over the other the way English does.

How Koreans would call you

Friends calling you over
베티야!
베티 ends in a vowel, so it takes 야. Casual, for close friends.
At work or with new people
베티 씨
베티 ssi. Polite, roughly "Mr" or "Ms", but used with the first name.
Customer, client, online
베티 님
베티 nim. What you'll see on a delivery box or a booking.

Korean given names are almost always two syllables, so 베티 already sounds natural to Korean ears. With foreign names some Koreans skip 야 and just say 베티 or 베티 씨.

Why it's spelled 베티

Here the letters and the sound line up: applying the loanword rules letter by letter already gives 베티, the same spelling Korean media use.

  • Double letters count once. Hangul spells the sound, not the letters.

Questions people ask

How do you write Betty in Korean?

Betty is written 베티 in Hangul (Revised Romanization: beti). It is the standard spelling Korean media use for the name. In Hangul it has two syllables: 베 + 티.

How do you pronounce Betty in Korean?

Say it be-ti, with even weight on every syllable. ㅂ at the start is soft and unvoiced, halfway between p and b.

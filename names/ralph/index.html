Ralph in Korean 랠프

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Ralph as 랠프: two syllables, said rael-peu. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Ralph in Hangul
랠프
Revised Romanization: raelpeu · said rael-peu · two syllables

Ralph letter by letter

Each Hangul block is one syllable, built from a consonant, a vowel and sometimes a final consonant underneath.

랠
ㄹ r/l + ㅐ ae + ㄹ l (final)
rael
프
ㅍ p + ㅡ eu
peu
Pronunciation tip

The opening ㄹ is a single light tap, halfway between an English r and l. Never roll it.

  • 프 carries the filler vowel ㅡ (eu). Keep it short and light, almost just the consonant.
  • ㄹ at the bottom of 랠 is an l: the tongue touches just behind the teeth and stays there.
  • ㅐ (ae) is said like the e in "bed" by most Koreans today, a little more closed than the a in "cat".
  • Two even beats, rael-peu: Korean doesn't stress one syllable over the other the way English does.

How Koreans would call you

Friends calling you over
랠프야!
랠프 ends in a vowel, so it takes 야. Casual, for close friends.
At work or with new people
랠프 씨
랠프 ssi. Polite, roughly "Mr" or "Ms", but used with the first name.
Customer, client, online
랠프 님
랠프 nim. What you'll see on a delivery box or a booking.

Korean given names are almost always two syllables, so 랠프 already sounds natural to Korean ears. With foreign names some Koreans skip 야 and just say 랠프 or 랠프 씨.

Why it's spelled 랠프

Here the letters and the sound line up: applying the loanword rules letter by letter already gives 랠프, the same spelling Korean media use.

  • ph is an f sound, and Korean has no f, so it becomes ㅍ (p).
  • Korean adds the vowel ㅡ to consonants that have no vowel of their own (프), which is why Ralph has 2 syllables in Korean.

Questions people ask

How do you write Ralph in Korean?

Ralph is written 랠프 in Hangul (Revised Romanization: raelpeu). It is the standard spelling Korean media use for the name. In Hangul it has two syllables: 랠 + 프.

How do you pronounce Ralph in Korean?

Say it rael-peu, with even weight on every syllable. The opening ㄹ is a single light tap, halfway between an English r and l. Never roll it.

How to read this

These are typical windows from the last five springs, not a weather forecast. The Korea Meteorological Administration publishes its official prediction in early March; we update this page the same week. A warm March pulls everything four or five days earlier; a cold snap pushes it back. Peak bloom means the week from full bloom, when the trees look like the photos.

Where to go

SpotWhy
Yeouido Yunjung-ro, SeoulThe famous tunnel of trees by the National Assembly. Festival crowds, worth one evening visit. Yeouinaru or National Assembly Station.
Seokchon Lake, SeoulA loop around the lake under Lotte Tower. Less crowded before 9 am. Jamsil Station.
Gyeongbokgung and Samcheong-dongPalace walls plus blossoms. Buy the palace ticket and go early.
Jinhae, ChangwonKorea's biggest blossom festival (Gunhangje), usually the last week of March into early April. Yeojwacheon stream and Gyeonghwa Station. Day trip from Busan.
GyeongjuBomun Lake and the historic sites lined with trees. Quieter than Jinhae, more photogenic than Seoul.
JejuKing cherry trees, the biggest blossoms in Korea. Jeonnong-ro in Jeju City, late March.

Fall foliage, the other season

If you miss spring, autumn does the same trick in reverse: Seoraksan turns first around the first week of October, Seoul's ginkgo and maples peak in the first half of November, and the south (Naejangsan, Jirisan) follows. Nami Island and Deoksugung's ginkgo road are the Seoul-side classics.

Why three ages?

For most of its history Korea counted age the East Asian way: you are one the day you are born, and everyone turns a year older together on New Year's Day. A baby born on 31 December is two years old the next morning. That is the Korean age (세는 나이, "counting age").

The law has always used international age (만 나이, "full age") for official matters, and on 28 June 2023 the government made it the single standard for documents, medical care and contracts. But habits are slower than laws. Ask someone at a bar how old they are and you will still hear the Korean age, because it decides who speaks casually to whom.

Then there is year age (연 나이): the current year minus your birth year. It exists so that a whole school year becomes adults together. It still sets the legal age for alcohol, tobacco and conscription. If you were born in 2007, you can drink from 1 January 2026, even if your birthday is in December.

Questions people ask

Did Korea abolish Korean age?

Officially, yes. Since 28 June 2023 international age is the legal standard. Socially, Korean age is fading but still common, especially among people over thirty. Year age was never abolished and still governs drinking and conscription.

Which age should I say when someone asks?

Say your international age and add the year you were born ("I'm 27, born in 1999"). The birth year is what Koreans actually use to work out the relationship, and it removes any confusion.

Why does a Korean website show me an age I have never been?

Older systems and some apps still display Korean age. If a number is one or two years higher than you expect, that is why.

Does the Korean age change on Lunar New Year?

No. Korean age changes on 1 January of the solar calendar. Lunar New Year (Seollal) is a holiday, not an age reset.

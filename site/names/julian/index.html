Julian in Korean 줄리언

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Julian as 줄리언: three syllables, said jul-li-eon. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Pronunciation tip

The double ㄹ in 줄리 is a clear English l, as in "hello", not an r.

  • ㅓ (eo) in 언 is the open "uh" of "sun", not an "o".
  • ㅈ at the start is soft and unvoiced, halfway between ch and j.
  • ㅇ at the top of 언 is silent, so 언 is just the vowel: eon.
  • Give all 3 syllables the same weight. Korean has no strong stress, so it's jul-li-eon, evenly.

Korean given names are almost always two syllables (after a one-syllable family name), so at 3 syllables 줄리언 reads as a foreign name at a glance. In casual sentences Koreans often add 이 after a name that ends in a consonant: 줄리언이 왔어 ("Julian's here").

How Koreans write it

the settled media spelling

Spelled letter-by-letter you'd get 줄리안, but Korean media write 줄리언, because the name is written the way it sounds, not the way it's spelled.

  • An l between vowels is written twice (final ㄹ + ㄹ), so Koreans say a clear l instead of a tapped r.
  • The unstressed "uh" vowel is written ㅓ (eo), whatever letter English uses for it.

How do you pronounce Julian in Korean?

Say it jul-li-eon, with even weight on every syllable. The double ㄹ in 줄리 is a clear English l, as in "hello", not an r.

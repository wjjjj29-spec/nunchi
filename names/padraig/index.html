Pádraig in Korean 포리그

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Pádraig as 포리그: three syllables, said po-ri-geu. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Pronunciation tip

ㄹ between two vowels (포리) is one quick tap of the tongue, like the tt in American "butter": closer to a soft d than an English r.

  • 그 carries the filler vowel ㅡ (eu). Keep it short and light, almost just the consonant.
  • ㅍ at the start is aspirated: a clear puff of air, like the p in "pie".
  • ㅗ is a pure "o" with rounded lips. Don't add the w-glide English puts in "go".
  • Give all 3 syllables the same weight. Korean has no strong stress, so it's po-ri-geu, evenly.

Customer, client, online
포리그 님
포리그 nim. What you'll see on a delivery box or a booking.

Korean given names are almost always two syllables (after a one-syllable family name), so at 3 syllables 포리그 reads as a foreign name at a glance. With foreign names some Koreans skip 야 and just say 포리그 or 포리그 씨.

Spelled letter-by-letter you'd get 패드레이그, but Korean media write 포리그, because the name is written the way it sounds in Irish, not the way it's spelled.

  • It is an Irish (Gaelic) name, and Irish spelling hides the sound: several vowels are silent. Korean writes what you hear, not the letters.

Questions people ask

How do you write Pádraig in Korean?

Pádraig is written 포리그 in Hangul (Revised Romanization: porigeu). It is the standard spelling Korean media use for the name and follows the Irish pronunciation. In Hangul it has three syllables: 포 + 리 + 그.

How do you pronounce Pádraig in Korean?

Say it po-ri-geu, with even weight on every syllable. ㄹ between two vowels (포리) is one quick tap of the tongue, like the tt in American "butter": closer to a soft d than an English r.

Jason in Korean 제이슨

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Jason as 제이슨: three syllables, said je-i-seun. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Pronunciation tip

슨 carries the filler vowel ㅡ (eu). Keep it short and light, almost just the consonant.

  • ㅈ at the start is soft and unvoiced, halfway between ch and j.
  • ㅇ at the top of 이 is silent, so 이 is just the vowel: i.
  • Give all 3 syllables the same weight. Korean has no strong stress, so it's je-i-seun, evenly.

제이슨 nim. What you'll see on a delivery box or a booking.

Korean given names are almost always two syllables (after a one-syllable family name), so at 3 syllables 제이슨 reads as a foreign name at a glance. In casual sentences Koreans often add 이 after a name that ends in a consonant: 제이슨이 왔어 ("Jason's here").

Why it's spelled 제이슨

How Koreans write it
제이슨
the settled media spelling

Spelled letter-by-letter you'd get 재손, but Korean media write 제이슨, because the name is written the way it sounds, not the way it's spelled.

  • The long a (as in "day") is spelled as two beats, 제이, because Korean writes each part of the diphthong.
  • Korean adds the vowel ㅡ to consonants that have no vowel of their own (슨), which is why Jason has 3 syllables in Korean.

Questions people ask

How do you write Jason in Korean?

Jason is written 제이슨 in Hangul (Revised Romanization: jeiseun). It is the standard spelling Korean media use for the name. In Hangul it has three syllables: 제 + 이 + 슨.

How do you pronounce Jason in Korean?

Say it je-i-seun, with even weight on every syllable. 슨 carries the filler vowel ㅡ (eu). Keep it short and light, almost just the consonant.

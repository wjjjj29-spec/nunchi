Nurul in Korean 누룰

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Nurul as 누룰: two syllables, said nu-rul. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Nurul letter by letter

Each Hangul block is one syllable, built from a consonant, a vowel and sometimes a final consonant underneath.

nu
룰

Pronunciation tip

ㄹ between two vowels (누룰) is one quick tap of the tongue, like the tt in American "butter": closer to a soft d than an English r.

  • ㄹ at the bottom of 룰 is an l: the tongue touches just behind the teeth and stays there.
  • Two even beats, nu-rul: Korean doesn't stress one syllable over the other the way English does.

Korean given names are almost always two syllables, so 누룰 already sounds natural to Korean ears. In casual sentences Koreans often add 이 after a name that ends in a consonant: 누룰이 왔어 ("Nurul's here").

Questions people ask

How do you write Nurul in Korean?

Nurul is written 누룰 in Hangul (Revised Romanization: nurul). It is the standard spelling Korean media use for the name. In Hangul it has two syllables: 누 + 룰.

How do you pronounce Nurul in Korean?

Say it nu-rul, with even weight on every syllable. ㄹ between two vowels (누룰) is one quick tap of the tongue, like the tt in American "butter": closer to a soft d than an English r.

What does Nurul mean in Korean?

On its own, nothing: 누룰 spells the sound of Nurul, so the syllables are not Korean words and Koreans read it simply as a foreign name.

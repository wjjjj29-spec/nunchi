Your first trip to Busan: getting there, getting around, what to see

2½ hours from Seoul by KTX. Which neighbourhood to stay in, how the metro works, and how to fit Gamcheon and Yonggungsa into two days.

4 min readChecked 29 October 2026
At a glance1 / 6
The short version
  • Getting there: KTX from Seoul Station or Suseo, about 2½ hours, ₩54,400 standard since the September 2026 fare cut. Flying in, Gimhae Airport links to the metro by light rail.
  • Getting around: metro fares are ₩1,600–1,800 with a transport card, and Seoul T-money works. Busan is spread out, so pick your base carefully.
  • Where to stay: Haeundae or Gwangalli for the sea, Nampo-dong or Seomyeon for food, markets and short stays.
  • How long: two days covers Haeundae, Gwangalli, Gamcheon and Haedong Yonggungsa. Spring and autumn are the most comfortable seasons.

Getting there

From Seoul, take the KTX. Trains run from Seoul Station, and from Suseo if you're staying in Gangnam, to Busan Station in about 2½ hours, depending on stops. The standard Seoul–Busan fare is ₩54,400. It was ₩59,800 until the KTX–SRT merger on 1 September 2026, and that old number is still all over the internet. Tickets open two months ahead, and Friday evening and Sunday afternoon trains sell out. How to book with a foreign card: KTX booking for foreigners.

Flying in? Gimhae International Airport (PUS) sits on the western edge of the city. The Busan–Gimhae Light Rail links it to Metro Line 2 at Sasang and Line 3 at Daejeo. It's the cheapest way in, but the Sasang transfer means stairs and a long walk, so with big bags the airport bus or a taxi is easier.

The metro and where to stay

Busan Metro fares with a transport card are ₩1,600 for trips up to 10km and ₩1,800 beyond. Single-journey QR tickets cost ₩100 more. Your Seoul T-money card works here, and transfers to city buses within 30 minutes are discounted. Busan is long and thin, so where you stay matters more than in Seoul. Busan Station to Haeundae takes about 50–60 minutes, with a change at Seomyeon.

First trip and here for the sea? Stay in Haeundae or Gwangalli. Here for food and markets, or on a short stay? Nampo-dong or Seomyeon. The Gwangalli drone show is free every Saturday, about 10 minutes long, at 7pm and 9pm from October to February and 8pm and 10pm from March to September. Weather can cancel it.

Nunchi tip

Taxi drivers rarely need an address for the big names. Show or say 해운대 해수욕장 가 주세요 (Haeundae haesuyokjang ga juseyo, "Haeundae Beach, please"). More in our taxis in Korea guide.

Gamcheon Culture Village

감천문화마을 (Gamcheon munhwa maeul, Gamcheon culture village) is a hillside of pastel houses first built by Korean War refugees and repainted by residents and artists in a 2009 art project. Entry is free. It's open 9am–6pm from March to October and 9am–5pm from November to February. People live here, so keep your voice down and don't fly drones.

Take Line 1 to Toseong, Exit 6, then village bus Saha 1-1, Seo-gu 2 or Seo-gu 2-2 to Gamjeong Elementary School (감정초등학교), and walk five minutes. Buy the stamp map at the information centre and follow the alleys. The Gamcheon Alley Festival runs on 31 October and 1 November 2026, 10am–6pm. Pair it with Nampo-dong and Jagalchi the same day, as they're close.

Haedong Yonggungsa

해동용궁사 (Haedong Yonggungsa, Dragon Palace temple) is a Buddhist temple built on rocks above the sea in Gijang, northeast of Haeundae. It's free and open every day 4:30am–7:20pm, last entry 6:50pm. From Haeundae Station Exit 7, take bus 181, 100 or Haeundae 9 to the Haedong Yonggungsa stop. Or take the Donghae Line to Osiria Station, Exit 1, and bus 139. It's a working temple: talk quietly, and use the side doors of the main hall, as the front door is for monks.

When to go

  • Spring and autumn are the easy seasons: mild, clear and good for walking the coast.
  • Summer is hot and humid and the beaches are packed. In 2026 Haeundae's official swimming season ran from 26 June to 15 September, 9am–6pm. Typhoons are most likely in August and September.
  • Winter is milder than Seoul's but windy by the water. Fewer crowds, cheaper hotels.
  • Busan Fireworks Festival: Saturday 7 November 2026 from 7pm, over Gwangalli. The beach is free but full hours ahead. Paid seats are sold on NOL Ticket. Hotels book out, so reserve early.

How many days?

Two days covers the highlights: one day east (Haeundae, Yonggungsa, Gwangalli at night) and one day west (Gamcheon, Jagalchi, Nampo-dong). Three days lets you add Seomyeon, a slow beach morning and a second look at whatever you liked. A day trip from Seoul works on paper, but it's about five hours of trains for half a day in the city. Questions people ask

How long is the KTX from Seoul to Busan and how much is it?

About 2½ hours from Seoul Station, depending on stops. The standard fare has been ₩54,400 since the KTX and SRT merged on 1 September 2026, down from ₩59,800.

How do I get from Gimhae Airport to Haeundae?

The simplest way is the airport bus, about ₩9,500 with no transfer. A taxi is about ₩35,000. The light rail and metro is cheaper but involves a transfer and takes longer.

Where should I stay in Busan for the first time?

Haeundae or Gwangalli if you want the beach and sea views, Nampo-dong or Seomyeon if you want markets, food and an easy base for Gamcheon.

How many days do you need in Busan?

Two days covers the main sights: Haeundae, Haedong Yonggungsa and Gwangalli on one day, Gamcheon, Jagalchi and Nampo-dong on the other. Three days is more relaxed.

What time is the Gwangalli drone show?

Every Saturday, free, about 10 minutes long: 7pm and 9pm from October to February, 8pm and 10pm from March to September. It can be cancelled for weather.

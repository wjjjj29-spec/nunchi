Anne in Korean 앤

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Anne as 앤: one syllable, said aen. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Anne in Hangul
앤
Revised Romanization: aen · said aen · one syllable

Same Hangul as Ann: Korean spells the sound, so different English spellings can land on the same 앤.

Anne letter by letter

Each Hangul block is one syllable, built from a consonant, a vowel and sometimes a final consonant underneath.

앤
ㅇ silent + ㅐ ae + ㄴ n (final)
aen
Pronunciation tip

ㅐ (ae) is said like the e in "bed" by most Koreans today, a little more closed than the a in "cat".

  • It's a single syllable in Korean, so say it in one short beat: aen.

How Koreans would call you

Friends calling you over
앤아!
앤 ends in a consonant, so it takes 아, said 애나. Casual, for close friends.
At work or with new people
앤 씨
앤 ssi. Polite, roughly "Mr" or "Ms", but used with the first name.
Customer, client, online
앤 님
앤 nim. What you'll see on a delivery box or a booking.

One syllable is short for Korea, where given names are almost always two, so 앤 stands out and is easy to remember. In casual sentences Koreans often add 이 after a name that ends in a consonant: 앤이 왔어 ("Anne's here").

Spelled letter-by-letter you'd get 애네, but Korean media write 앤, because the name is written the way it sounds, not the way it's spelled.

  • Double letters count once. Hangul spells the sound, not the letters.

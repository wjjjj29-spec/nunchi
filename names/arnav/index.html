Arnav in Korean 아르나브

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Arnav as 아르나브: four syllables, said a-reu-na-beu. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Pronunciation tip

ㄹ between two vowels (아르) is one quick tap of the tongue, like the tt in American "butter": closer to a soft d than an English r.

  • 르 and 브 carry the filler vowel ㅡ (eu). Keep them short and light, almost just the consonant.
  • Give all 4 syllables the same weight. Korean has no strong stress, so it's a-reu-na-beu, evenly.

Korean given names are almost always two syllables (after a one-syllable family name), so at 4 syllables 아르나브 reads as a foreign name at a glance. With foreign names some Koreans skip 야 and just say 아르나브 or 아르나브 씨.

Spelled letter-by-letter you'd get 아내브, but Korean media write 아르나브, because the name is written the way it sounds, not the way it's spelled.

  • There is no v in Korean either: v becomes ㅂ (b).
  • Korean adds the vowel ㅡ to consonants that have no vowel of their own (르 and 브), which is why Arnav has 4 syllables in Korean.

Questions people ask

How do you write Arnav in Korean?

Arnav is written 아르나브 in Hangul (Revised Romanization: areunabeu). It is the standard spelling Korean media use for the name. In Hangul it has four syllables: 아 + 르 + 나 + 브.

How do you pronounce Arnav in Korean?

Say it a-reu-na-beu, with even weight on every syllable. ㄹ between two vowels (아르) is one quick tap of the tongue, like the tt in American "butter": closer to a soft d than an English r.

What does Arnav mean in Korean?

On its own, nothing: 아르나브 spells the sound of Arnav, so the syllables are not Korean words and Koreans read it simply as a foreign name.

Philip in Korean 필립

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Philip as 필립: two syllables, said pil-lip. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Philip in Hangul
필립
Revised Romanization: pillip · said pil-lip · two syllables

Philip letter by letter

Each Hangul block is one syllable, built from a consonant, a vowel and sometimes a final consonant underneath.

필
ㅍ p + ㅣ i + ㄹ l (final)
pil
립
ㄹ l + ㅣ i + ㅂ p (final)
lip
Pronunciation tip

The double ㄹ in 필립 is a clear English l, as in "hello", not an r.

  • The final ㅂ in 립 is unreleased: stop the sound (p) without a puff of air.
  • ㅍ at the start is aspirated: a clear puff of air, like the p in "pie".
  • Two even beats, pil-lip: Korean doesn't stress one syllable over the other the way English does.

Korean given names are almost always two syllables, so 필립 already sounds natural to Korean ears. In casual sentences Koreans often add 이 after a name that ends in a consonant: 필립이 왔어 ("Philip's here").

Why it's spelled 필립

Here the letters and the sound line up: applying the loanword rules letter by letter already gives 필립, the same spelling Korean media use.

  • ph is an f sound, and Korean has no f, so it becomes ㅍ (p).
  • An l between vowels is written twice (final ㄹ + ㄹ), so Koreans say a clear l instead of a tapped r.

Questions people ask

How do you write Philip in Korean?

Philip is written 필립 in Hangul (Revised Romanization: pillip). It is the standard spelling Korean media use for the name. In Hangul it has two syllables: 필 + 립.

How do you pronounce Philip in Korean?

Say it pil-lip, with even weight on every syllable. The double ㄹ in 필립 is a clear English l, as in "hello", not an r.

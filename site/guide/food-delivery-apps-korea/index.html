Food delivery in Korea: which apps work for foreigners

Baemin now works with a foreign number and card. Here's how to order, write a Korean address and get it left at the door.

4 min readChecked 29 October 2026
At a glance1 / 6
The short version
  • Use Baemin: English menus, a text code to your foreign number, and foreign cards, Apple Pay, Alipay+ or WeChat Pay. No Korean number needed in 2026.
  • Coupang Eats and Yogiyo: both have English and take foreign cards, but sign-up still expects a Korean mobile number.
  • Fees: check the minimum order and the delivery fee (배달팁), usually about ₩0–4,000. There's no extra tip for the rider.
  • Address: paste the Korean address from Naver Map, add 동 building and 호 unit, and write 문 앞에 두고 가 주세요 to have it left at the door.

Which app works for foreigners

Use Baemin (배달의민족 Baedal Minjok, "delivery nation"), Korea's biggest delivery app. It's the only one a visitor can reliably use from start to finish: English, Japanese and Chinese since February 2026, foreign cards, and checkout with an overseas phone number verified by a text code, rolled out over summer 2026. Coupang Eats and Yogiyo have English screens and take foreign cards too, but sign-up still expects a Korean mobile number.

Setting up Baemin

  • Language: if your phone isn't set to Korean, the app offers to switch to English, Japanese or Chinese. Search, menus, payment and live delivery tracking are all translated.
  • Your number: pick your country code and enter the code sent by text. You can also log in with a Google account. Residents can make a full account with a Korean number and their residence card number.
  • Payment: add a foreign Visa or Mastercard, Apple Pay (added May 2026), or Alipay+ and WeChat Pay. Some foreign cards still get declined, so keep a second one ready.

Menus are translated by AI, so dish names can come out strange. If a translation makes no sense, check the photo or paste the Korean into Papago. More visitor apps are in our Korea travel apps guide.

Delivery fees and minimum orders

Before you pay, check three things on the order screen:

  • Minimum order (최소주문금액): many restaurants won't accept an order below a set total, so solo diners sometimes add a drink or a side.
  • Delivery fee (배달팁 baedal tip): set by the restaurant and the app, and it changes with distance and delivery type. It's usually about ₩0–4,000. Faster single-order delivery costs more than slower shared delivery. The "tip" is the fee itself; you don't hand the rider anything extra.
  • Final total in won: your card will charge this, plus any foreign transaction fee from your bank. See cards and cash in Korea for which cards cost least.

Living here? Baemin Club and Coupang's WOW membership cut or waive delivery fees for subscribers, and Coupang Eats has run free delivery for all members as a promotion. Prices change often, so compare in the apps.

Writing your address in Korean

Riders work from the Korean road-name address (도로명주소 doromyeong juso). English spellings often don't match, so search or paste the address in Korean.

  • Copy it from Naver Map: tap your building, copy the Korean address and paste it into the app.
  • Building and unit: apartments use 동 (dong, building) and 호 (ho, unit). 101동 1203호 means building 101, flat 1203.
  • Small buildings: for a one-room or officetel (오피스텔), add the building name and floor.
  • Door code: put the main entrance code (공동현관 비밀번호 gongdong hyeongwan bimilbeonho) in the note for the rider, or say you'll come down.

Leaving it at the door

Contact-free drop-off is normal in Korea. Type the request into the rider note, and the app tells you when the order arrives.

Ordering to a hotel

Use the hotel's exact Korean address from Naver Map, or ask the front desk to show it to you, then add the hotel name and your room number. Ask at check-in whether riders can come up to the rooms. If not, choose front-desk drop-off or meet the rider in the lobby. Guesthouses and short-let flats often have a door code; put it in the rider note.

Nunchi tip

Search by dish, not by restaurant. Most places are listed under Korean names, but typing chicken, pizza or tteokbokki in English works. Chicken is the top pick: 27% of foreign users' Baemin orders from January to July 2026.

Chicken by the Han River

Ordering fried chicken and beer to a Han River park is a Seoul classic. Riders can't find a picnic blanket, so the parks have designated delivery pickup zones (배달존 baedal jon). Set the zone as your address and meet the rider there. In 2026 Seoul and Baemin added signs and maps in several languages at Yeouido and Ttukseom Han River Parks to make this easier for visitors.

Questions people ask

Can I order food delivery in Korea without a Korean phone number?

Yes, with Baemin. Since summer 2026 it lets visitors verify an overseas number by text code, or log in with Google, and pay with a foreign card. Coupang Eats and Yogiyo still expect a Korean number.

Does Baemin accept foreign credit cards?

Yes. Baemin takes foreign Visa and Mastercard, Apple Pay (since May 2026), Alipay+ and WeChat Pay. Some foreign cards are still declined, so keep a backup.

How much is delivery in Korea?

The delivery fee is set per restaurant and changes with distance and delivery type, usually about ₩0–4,000. Many restaurants also have a minimum order. Riders don't expect a tip on top.

Can I get food delivered to my hotel in Korea?

Yes. Use the hotel's Korean address from Naver Map, add the hotel name and room number, and ask at check-in whether riders can come up. If not, choose front-desk drop-off or meet them in the lobby.

How do I say leave it at the door in Korean?

Write 문 앞에 두고 가 주세요 (mun ape dugo ga juseyo) in the rider note. Add 벨 누르지 말아 주세요 if you don't want the bell rung.

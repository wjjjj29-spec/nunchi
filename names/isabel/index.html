Isabel in Korean 이저벨

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Isabel as 이저벨: three syllables, said i-jeo-bel. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Pronunciation tip

ㄹ at the bottom of 벨 is an l: the tongue touches just behind the teeth and stays there.

  • ㅓ (eo) in 저 is the open "uh" of "sun", not an "o".
  • Give all 3 syllables the same weight. Korean has no strong stress, so it's i-jeo-bel, evenly.

How Koreans would call you

Friends calling you over
이저벨아!
이저벨 ends in a consonant, so it takes 아, said 이저베라. Casual, for close friends.
At work or with new people
이저벨 씨
이저벨 ssi. Polite, roughly "Mr" or "Ms", but used with the first name.
Customer, client, online
이저벨 님
이저벨 nim. What you'll see on a delivery box or a booking.

Korean given names are almost always two syllables (after a one-syllable family name), so at 3 syllables 이저벨 reads as a foreign name at a glance. In casual sentences Koreans often add 이 after a name that ends in a consonant: 이저벨이 왔어 ("Isabel's here").

How Koreans write it

Spelled letter-by-letter you'd get 이사벨, but Korean media write 이저벨, because the name is written the way it sounds, not the way it's spelled.

  • The unstressed "uh" vowel is written ㅓ (eo), whatever letter English uses for it.

Questions people ask

How do you write Isabel in Korean?

Isabel is written 이저벨 in Hangul (Revised Romanization: ijeobel). It is the standard spelling Korean media use for the name. In Hangul it has three syllables: 이 + 저 + 벨.

How do you pronounce Isabel in Korean?

Say it i-jeo-bel, with even weight on every syllable. ㄹ at the bottom of 벨 is an l: the tongue touches just behind the teeth and stays there.

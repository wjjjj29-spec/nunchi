Janet in Korean 재닛

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Janet as 재닛: two syllables, said jae-nit. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Pronunciation tip

The final ㅅ in 닛 is unreleased: stop the sound (t) without a puff of air. It is a t sound here, not s.

  • ㅐ (ae) is said like the e in "bed" by most Koreans today, a little more closed than the a in "cat".
  • ㅈ at the start is soft and unvoiced, halfway between ch and j.
  • Two even beats, jae-nit: Korean doesn't stress one syllable over the other the way English does.

재닛 nim. What you'll see on a delivery box or a booking.

Korean given names are almost always two syllables, so 재닛 already sounds natural to Korean ears. In casual sentences Koreans often add 이 after a name that ends in a consonant: 재닛이 왔어 ("Janet's here").

Spelled letter-by-letter you'd get 재넷, but Korean media write 재닛, because the name is written the way it sounds, not the way it's spelled.

  • A final t is written with ㅅ under the syllable. It's still said as a held t.

Questions people ask

How do you write Janet in Korean?

Janet is written 재닛 in Hangul (Revised Romanization: jaenit). It is the standard spelling Korean media use for the name. In Hangul it has two syllables: 재 + 닛.

How do you pronounce Janet in Korean?

Say it jae-nit, with even weight on every syllable. The final ㅅ in 닛 is unreleased: stop the sound (t) without a puff of air. It is a t sound here, not s.

Jim in Korean 짐

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Jim as 짐: one syllable, said jim. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Jim in Hangul
짐
Revised Romanization: jim · said jim · one syllable

Jim letter by letter

Each Hangul block is one syllable, built from a consonant, a vowel and sometimes a final consonant underneath.

짐
ㅈ j + ㅣ i + ㅁ m (final)
jim
Pronunciation tip

ㅈ at the start is soft and unvoiced, halfway between ch and j.

  • It's a single syllable in Korean, so say it in one short beat: jim.

How Koreans would call you

Friends calling you over
짐아!
짐 ends in a consonant, so it takes 아, said 지마. Casual, for close friends.
At work or with new people
짐 씨
짐 ssi. Polite, roughly "Mr" or "Ms", but used with the first name.
Customer, client, online
짐 님
짐 nim. What you'll see on a delivery box or a booking.

One syllable is short for Korea, where given names are almost always two, so 짐 stands out and is easy to remember. In casual sentences Koreans often add 이 after a name that ends in a consonant: 짐이 왔어 ("Jim's here").

Good to know

짐 means luggage or a burden in Korean. Friends will find this funnier than you do.

Why it's spelled 짐

Here the letters and the sound line up: applying the loanword rules letter by letter already gives 짐, the same spelling Korean media use.

Carter in Korean 카터

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Carter as 카터: two syllables, said ka-teo. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Pronunciation tip

ㅓ (eo) in 터 is the open "uh" of "sun", not an "o".

  • ㅋ at the start is aspirated: a clear puff of air, like the k in "kite".
  • Two even beats, ka-teo: Korean doesn't stress one syllable over the other the way English does.

How Koreans would call you

Friends calling you over
카터야!
카터 ends in a vowel, so it takes 야. Casual, for close friends.
At work or with new people
카터 씨
카터 ssi. Polite, roughly "Mr" or "Ms", but used with the first name.
Customer, client, online
카터 님
카터 nim. What you'll see on a delivery box or a booking.

Korean given names are almost always two syllables, so 카터 already sounds natural to Korean ears. With foreign names some Koreans skip 야 and just say 카터 or 카터 씨.

Why it's spelled 카터

Here the letters and the sound line up: applying the loanword rules letter by letter already gives 카터, the same spelling Korean media use.

  • An r after a vowel (before a consonant or at the end) isn't written. Korean spelling follows British-style pronunciation, where that r is silent.
  • The unstressed "uh" vowel is written ㅓ (eo), whatever letter English uses for it.

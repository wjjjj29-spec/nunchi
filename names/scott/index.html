Scott in Korean 스콧

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Scott as 스콧: two syllables, said seu-kot. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Scott in Hangul
스콧
Revised Romanization: seukot · said seu-kot · two syllables

Scott letter by letter

Each Hangul block is one syllable, built from a consonant, a vowel and sometimes a final consonant underneath.

스
ㅅ s + ㅡ eu
seu
콧
ㅋ k + ㅗ o + ㅅ t (final)
kot
Pronunciation tip

스 carries the filler vowel ㅡ (eu). Keep it short and light, almost just the consonant.

  • The final ㅅ in 콧 is unreleased: stop the sound (t) without a puff of air. It is a t sound here, not s.
  • ㅗ is a pure "o" with rounded lips. Don't add the w-glide English puts in "go".
  • Two even beats, seu-kot: Korean doesn't stress one syllable over the other the way English does.

스콧 ssi. Polite, roughly "Mr" or "Ms", but used with the first name.
Customer, client, online
스콧 님
스콧 nim. What you'll see on a delivery box or a booking.

Korean given names are almost always two syllables, so 스콧 already sounds natural to Korean ears. In casual sentences Koreans often add 이 after a name that ends in a consonant: 스콧이 왔어 ("Scott's here").

Why it's spelled 스콧

Here the letters and the sound line up: applying the loanword rules letter by letter already gives 스콧, the same spelling Korean media use.

  • A final t is written with ㅅ under the syllable. It's still said as a held t.
  • Double letters count once. Hangul spells the sound, not the letters.
  • Korean adds the vowel ㅡ to consonants that have no vowel of their own (스), which is why Scott has 2 syllables in Korean.

Questions people ask

How do you write Scott in Korean?

Scott is written 스콧 in Hangul (Revised Romanization: seukot). It is the standard spelling Korean media use for the name. In Hangul it has two syllables: 스 + 콧.

How do you pronounce Scott in Korean?

Say it seu-kot, with even weight on every syllable. 스 carries the filler vowel ㅡ (eu). Keep it short and light, almost just the consonant.

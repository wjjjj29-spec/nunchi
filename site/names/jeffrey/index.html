Jeffrey in Korean 제프리

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Jeffrey as 제프리: three syllables, said je-peu-ri. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Pronunciation tip

ㄹ between two vowels (프리) is one quick tap of the tongue, like the tt in American "butter": closer to a soft d than an English r.

  • 프 carries the filler vowel ㅡ (eu). Keep it short and light, almost just the consonant.
  • ㅈ at the start is soft and unvoiced, halfway between ch and j.
  • Give all 3 syllables the same weight. Korean has no strong stress, so it's je-peu-ri, evenly.

How Koreans would call you

At work or with new people
제프리 씨
제프리 ssi. Polite, roughly "Mr" or "Ms", but used with the first name.
Customer, client, online
제프리 님
제프리 nim. What you'll see on a delivery box or a booking.

Korean given names are almost always two syllables (after a one-syllable family name), so at 3 syllables 제프리 reads as a foreign name at a glance. With foreign names some Koreans skip 야 and just say 제프리 or 제프리 씨.

Why it's spelled 제프리

Here the letters and the sound line up: applying the loanword rules letter by letter already gives 제프리, the same spelling Korean media use.

  • Korean has no f sound, so f becomes ㅍ (p), said with a puff of air.
  • Double letters count once. Hangul spells the sound, not the letters.
  • Korean adds the vowel ㅡ to consonants that have no vowel of their own (프), which is why Jeffrey has 3 syllables in Korean.

Susan in Korean 수전

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Susan as 수전: two syllables, said su-jeon. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Susan in Hangul
수전
Revised Romanization: sujeon · said su-jeon · two syllables

Susan letter by letter

Each Hangul block is one syllable, built from a consonant, a vowel and sometimes a final consonant underneath.

ㅅ s + ㅜ u
su
전
ㅈ j + ㅓ eo + ㄴ n (final)
jeon
Pronunciation tip

ㅓ (eo) in 전 is the open "uh" of "sun", not an "o".

  • Two even beats, su-jeon: Korean doesn't stress one syllable over the other the way English does.

How Koreans would call you

Friends calling you over
수전아!
수전 ends in a consonant, so it takes 아, said 수저나. Casual, for close friends.
At work or with new people
수전 씨
수전 ssi. Polite, roughly "Mr" or "Ms", but used with the first name.
Customer, client, online
수전 님
수전 nim. What you'll see on a delivery box or a booking.

Korean given names are almost always two syllables, so 수전 already sounds natural to Korean ears. In casual sentences Koreans often add 이 after a name that ends in a consonant: 수전이 왔어 ("Susan's here").

Why it's spelled 수전

How Koreans write it
수전
the settled media spelling

Spelled letter-by-letter you'd get 수샌, but Korean media write 수전, because the name is written the way it sounds, not the way it's spelled.

  • The unstressed "uh" vowel is written ㅓ (eo), whatever letter English uses for it.

Questions people ask

How do you write Susan in Korean?

Susan is written 수전 in Hangul (Revised Romanization: sujeon). It is the standard spelling Korean media use for the name. In Hangul it has two syllables: 수 + 전.

How do you pronounce Susan in Korean?

Say it su-jeon, with even weight on every syllable. ㅓ (eo) in 전 is the open "uh" of "sun", not an "o".

Emergency numbers in Korea: who to call and what to say

119 for fire and ambulance, 112 for police, 1330 for an interpreter. How to get help in English, and what an ER visit costs.

The short version
  • 119: fire and ambulance, 24 hours. The ambulance is free, for visitors too. Say "English" and stay on the line for an interpreter.
  • 112: police, with English and Chinese interpreters around the clock since 2024.
  • 1330: the Korea Travel Hotline, 7am to midnight, can interpret on a three-way call. After midnight, Danuri on 1577-1366.
  • ER (응급실): open 24 hours, but minor cases wait longest and pay most. Tourists pay the full uninsured price, so carry travel insurance.

Dial 119 or 112 with no area code. If someone is badly hurt, in danger or there's a fire, call 119 or 112 first, not 1330.

Calling 119: fire and ambulance

The 119 ambulance is free, for visitors too. Seoul's 119 lists help in English, Chinese, Japanese, Mongolian and Vietnamese, and its staff also give first-aid instructions and hospital information over the phone. Say "English" clearly and stay on the line while they bring in an interpreter. Don't hang up if you hear a beep or silence while you're being connected.

The ambulance takes you to an emergency room. The ride is free, but the hospital bills you for treatment.

Have your location ready. The quickest ways to give it:

  • In a building: the Korean address from Naver Map or from your booking confirmation, plus the floor.
  • In the subway: the station name and the exit number you're nearest to.
  • On the street: the name of the shop, hotel or landmark you're standing next to.

Calling 112: police

Since March 2024 the police have kept English and Chinese interpreters on duty around the clock at the 112 centre, joining your call as a three-way conversation. Use 112 for a theft, an assault, a road accident or a lost child. For things you've simply left behind, see our lost items guide.

In central Seoul, uniformed tourist police patrol areas such as Myeongdong, Itaewon and Hongdae, and some officers speak English or Chinese. They're a good first stop for a scam or a dispute with a shop or taxi.

Interpretation lines

  • 1330 Korea Travel Hotline: free, 7am to midnight, in eight languages including English, Japanese, Chinese, Russian and Vietnamese. It can set up a three-way call with a hospital, the police or 119. From a foreign phone, dial +82-2-1330.
  • Danuri 1577-1366: 24-hour interpretation when 1330 is closed.
  • 1588-5644: a volunteer interpretation line in 17 languages, listed by the Seoul city government.
  • 1339: the Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency's 24-hour line. Ask whether you need an ER or where a late-night pharmacy is, with an interpreter on a three-way call.
  • 1345: the immigration helpline, 9am to 6pm in 20 languages, with English and Chinese until 10pm. For visa problems, not emergencies.

Emergency rooms (응급실)

An emergency room is an 응급실 (eunggeupsil), open 24 hours. Search the word on Naver Map, or use the ER finder on E-Gen, the national emergency medical site (Korean, but the map works). Patients are seen by how serious they are, not in order of arrival, so a minor problem can wait a long time.

Korea deliberately makes minor ER visits expensive. Since 13 September 2024, insured Korean patients judged minor or non-urgent at the larger hospital ERs (regional and local emergency centres) pay 90% of the bill themselves. The government's estimate put the average a patient pays at about ₩220,000 at regional emergency centres and about ₩100,000 at local ones. As a tourist you're not in national health insurance, so you pay the full price for everything. For a fever, a cold or a twisted ankle, a clinic or a pharmacy is far cheaper: see how to see a doctor in Korea and our Korean pharmacy guide.

Bring your passport and a card, and expect to pay before you leave. If you're admitted, the hospital may ask for a 보호자 (bohoja, guardian) to help with basic care. Paid carers (간병인 ganbyeongin) can be hired, but they rarely speak English.

Nunchi tip

Before leaving, ask for the receipt (영수증) and the itemised bill (진료비 세부내역서). Your travel insurer will want both, and getting them later from abroad is much harder.

Your embassy and travel insurance

Save your embassy's emergency number before you fly; most list a 24-hour line on their website. The US Embassy in Seoul, for example, uses 02-397-4114 after hours. Embassies can issue an emergency travel document if your passport is lost or stolen, help if you're arrested or in hospital, contact your family with your consent and give you lists of local lawyers and interpreters. They can't pay your bills, give you money or get you out of trouble with the law.

That's why travel insurance matters. Medical care in Korea is good but can be expensive, and visitors pay the uninsured rate. Get a policy that covers treatment in Korea and medical evacuation, keep the insurer's 24-hour number with your passport and call them as soon as you're admitted.

Questions people ask

What is the emergency number in Korea?

119 for fire and ambulance, 112 for police. Both work 24 hours with no area code. For interpretation and travel help, call 1330 (7am to midnight).

Is an ambulance free in Korea for tourists?

Yes. The public 119 ambulance is free for everyone. The hospital charges separately for treatment, and visitors pay the full uninsured price.

Do Korean emergency services speak English?

119 and 112 can bring an interpreter into the call. Say "English" and stay on the line. The police have had 24-hour English and Chinese interpreters at 112 since March 2024.

How much does an emergency room cost in Korea?

It depends on treatment. Korea makes minor ER visits expensive on purpose: after the 2024 change, even insured Korean patients with minor cases were expected to pay about ₩220,000 on average at regional emergency centres. Tourists pay the full uninsured price.

What can my embassy do in an emergency?

Issue an emergency travel document, help if you're arrested or in hospital, contact family and give you lists of lawyers and interpreters. Embassies can't pay medical bills, so get travel insurance.

Brian in Korean 브라이언

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Brian as 브라이언: four syllables, said beu-ra-i-eon. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Pronunciation tip

ㄹ between two vowels (브라) is one quick tap of the tongue, like the tt in American "butter": closer to a soft d than an English r.

  • 브 carries the filler vowel ㅡ (eu). Keep it short and light, almost just the consonant.
  • ㅓ (eo) in 언 is the open "uh" of "sun", not an "o".
  • ㅂ at the start is soft and unvoiced, halfway between p and b.
  • ㅇ at the top of 이 is silent, so 이 is just the vowel: i.

Korean given names are almost always two syllables (after a one-syllable family name), so at 4 syllables 브라이언 reads as a foreign name at a glance. In casual sentences Koreans often add 이 after a name that ends in a consonant: 브라이언이 왔어 ("Brian's here").

Why it's spelled 브라이언

How Koreans write it

Spelled letter-by-letter you'd get 브리앤, but Korean media write 브라이언, because the name is written the way it sounds, not the way it's spelled.

  • The long i (as in "my") is spelled 아이: two beats in Korean, one in English.
  • The unstressed "uh" vowel is written ㅓ (eo), whatever letter English uses for it.
  • Korean adds the vowel ㅡ to consonants that have no vowel of their own (브), which is why Brian has 4 syllables in Korean.

Questions people ask

How do you write Brian in Korean?

Brian is written 브라이언 in Hangul (Revised Romanization: beuraieon). It is the standard spelling Korean media use for the name. In Hangul it has four syllables: 브 + 라 + 이 + 언.

How do you pronounce Brian in Korean?

Say it beu-ra-i-eon, with even weight on every syllable. ㄹ between two vowels (브라) is one quick tap of the tongue, like the tt in American "butter": closer to a soft d than an English r.

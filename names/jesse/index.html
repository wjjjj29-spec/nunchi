Jesse in Korean 제시

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Jesse as 제시: two syllables, said je-si. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Jesse in Hangul
제시

Jesse letter by letter

Each Hangul block is one syllable, built from a consonant, a vowel and sometimes a final consonant underneath.

Pronunciation tip

ㅈ at the start is soft and unvoiced, halfway between ch and j.

  • Two even beats, je-si: Korean doesn't stress one syllable over the other the way English does.

How Koreans would call you

Friends calling you over
제시야!
제시 ends in a vowel, so it takes 야. Casual, for close friends.
At work or with new people
제시 씨
제시 ssi. Polite, roughly "Mr" or "Ms", but used with the first name.
Customer, client, online
제시 님
제시 nim. What you'll see on a delivery box or a booking.

Korean given names are almost always two syllables, so 제시 already sounds natural to Korean ears. With foreign names some Koreans skip 야 and just say 제시 or 제시 씨.

Spelled letter-by-letter you'd get 제세, but Korean media write 제시, because the name is written the way it sounds, not the way it's spelled.

  • Double letters count once. Hangul spells the sound, not the letters.

Questions people ask

How do you write Jesse in Korean?

Jesse is written 제시 in Hangul (Revised Romanization: jesi). It is the standard spelling Korean media use for the name. In Hangul it has two syllables: 제 + 시.

How do you pronounce Jesse in Korean?

Say it je-si, with even weight on every syllable. ㅈ at the start is soft and unvoiced, halfway between ch and j.

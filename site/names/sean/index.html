One syllable is short for Korea, where given names are almost always two, so 숀 stands out and is easy to remember. In casual sentences Koreans often add 이 after a name that ends in a consonant: 숀이 왔어 ("Sean's here").

How Koreans write it
숀
the settled media spelling

Spelled letter-by-letter you'd get 신, but Korean media write 숀, because the name is written the way it sounds in Irish, not the way it's spelled.

  • It is an Irish (Gaelic) name, and Irish spelling hides the sound: s before e or i is a sh sound and several vowels are silent. Korean writes what you hear, not the letters.

Christine in Korean 크리스틴

Pronunciation tip

ㄹ between two vowels (크리) is one quick tap of the tongue, like the tt in American "butter": closer to a soft d than an English r.

  • 크 and 스 carry the filler vowel ㅡ (eu). Keep them short and light, almost just the consonant.
  • ㅋ at the start is aspirated: a clear puff of air, like the k in "kite".
  • Give all 4 syllables the same weight. Korean has no strong stress, so it's keu-ri-seu-tin, evenly.

Korean given names are almost always two syllables (after a one-syllable family name), so at 4 syllables 크리스틴 reads as a foreign name at a glance. In casual sentences Koreans often add 이 after a name that ends in a consonant: 크리스틴이 왔어 ("Christine's here").

Why it's spelled 크리스틴

How Koreans write it
크리스틴
the settled media spelling

Spelled letter-by-letter you'd get 크리스타인, but Korean media write 크리스틴, because the name is written the way it sounds, not the way it's spelled.

  • The ch in Christine is a k sound, so Korean uses ㅋ (k), not ㅊ.
  • The final e is silent. It only makes the vowel before it long.
  • Korean adds the vowel ㅡ to consonants that have no vowel of their own (크 and 스), which is why Christine has 4 syllables in Korean.

How do you write Christine in Korean?

How do you pronounce Christine in Korean?

Say it keu-ri-seu-tin, with even weight on every syllable. ㄹ between two vowels (크리) is one quick tap of the tongue, like the tt in American "butter": closer to a soft d than an English r.

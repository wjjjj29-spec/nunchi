Isabella in Korean 이저벨라

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Isabella as 이저벨라: four syllables, said i-jeo-bel-la. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Pronunciation tip

The double ㄹ in 벨라 is a clear English l, as in "hello", not an r.

  • ㅓ (eo) in 저 is the open "uh" of "sun", not an "o".
  • Give all 4 syllables the same weight. Korean has no strong stress, so it's i-jeo-bel-la, evenly.

Korean given names are almost always two syllables (after a one-syllable family name), so at 4 syllables 이저벨라 reads as a foreign name at a glance. With foreign names some Koreans skip 야 and just say 이저벨라 or 이저벨라 씨.

Why it's spelled 이저벨라

How Koreans write it
이저벨라
the settled media spelling

Spelled letter-by-letter you'd get 이사벨라, but Korean media write 이저벨라, because the name is written the way it sounds, not the way it's spelled.

  • An l between vowels is written twice (final ㄹ + ㄹ), so Koreans say a clear l instead of a tapped r.
  • The unstressed "uh" vowel is written ㅓ (eo), whatever letter English uses for it.

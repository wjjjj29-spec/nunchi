Lost your phone, passport or wallet in Korea? Here's what to do

In Korea, lost things usually come back. Left on the subway, in a taxi or at the airport, here's where it goes and how to get it.

3 min readChecked 18 October 2026
At a glance1 / 6
The short version
  • Check Lost112 first. It's the police's national lost and found site, with an English section. Transport operators hand over unclaimed items after about a week.
  • Subway: tell station staff the time, platform and car right away. Seoul Metro's lost and found centres are at City Hall, Chungmuro, Wangsimni and Taereung.
  • Taxi: app rides show the driver's contact; if you paid by card, T-money (1644-1188) can trace the taxi.
  • Passport: report it to the police, then contact your embassy for an emergency passport. You can't leave Korea without one.

First: it really might come back

Korea is famous for this. Phones left in cafés and wallets dropped on the subway are regularly handed in, because finding and keeping someone's things is a crime here and CCTV is everywhere. Retrace your steps, then work through the places below.

Lost112: the national lost and found

Lost112 is run by the Korean National Police Agency and has an English section. Search found items by category (phone, wallet, bag), area and date. Each result tells you where the item is being held and how to claim it.

Timing matters: subway, bus and taxi operators usually keep items for about a week before handing unclaimed ones to the police, and that's when they appear on Lost112. So check the transport operator first, then Lost112 after a few days. To report a loss, call 182 (the police civil service line) or use the site. Bring your passport when you collect anything.

Left on the subway

  • Right away: tell staff at the nearest station the time, the platform and roughly which car. They can radio ahead.
  • Seoul Metro lost and found centres: City Hall (Lines 1–2, 02-6110-1122), Chungmuro (Lines 3–4, 02-6110-3344), Wangsimni (Lines 5 and 8) and Taereung Entrance (Lines 6–7). They're open weekdays 9am–6pm, and items are kept for about a week. General enquiries: Seoul Metro 1577-1234.
  • KTX and Korail trains: report it on the Korail website or app, or call 1544-7788, with your train number and seat.

Left in a taxi

  • Booked with an app (Kakao T, k.ride, Uber): the driver's contact and the plate number are in your ride history.
  • Paid by card: call T-money on 1644-1188 (or Cashbee on 1644-1472) with your card details, and they can find the taxi and the driver's number.
  • Got a receipt: it shows the plate number and the taxi company's phone number.
  • No idea which taxi: in Seoul, call 120 (Dasan Call Center). Press 9 for foreign languages, then 1 for English, weekdays 9am–6pm. After a week, check Lost112.

Left at Incheon Airport

The airport's lost and found is open daily 7am–10pm: Terminal 1 on the basement floor (032-741-3110) and Terminal 2 at the 2F one-stop service area (032-741-8988). Items left on a plane go to your airline instead.

Lost your passport

  1. Report it to the police. Go to any police station (or call 112) and ask for a loss report confirmation. Your embassy, your insurer and immigration may all ask for it.
  2. Contact your embassy. Only your own country can issue an emergency passport or travel document. Many embassies in Seoul can do one in a few working days.
  3. Before you fly, call 1345, the Immigration Contact Center, to check what you need. You can't pass departure immigration without a valid passport or travel document. If you live in Korea on a residence card, you must also report your new passport within 15 days.

Lost your phone or cards

  • Phone: use Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) from a friend's phone, and file a police loss report if you'll claim on insurance.
  • Bank cards: call your bank's emergency number from home to freeze them.
  • WOWPASS: pause the card in the app. A WOWPASS kiosk can issue a new card with your balance once you verify it's yours.
  • T-money: an unregistered card's balance usually can't be recovered, so don't load more than you need.
Nunchi tip

Take a photo of your passport's photo page and keep your accommodation's address in Korean on your phone. Both make the police report, the embassy visit and the taxi call ten times faster.

Getting help in English

1330, the Korea Travel Hotline, is free and can interpret for you (from a foreign phone: +82-2-1330). 112 police can set up a three-way call with an interpreter, and Danuri (1577-1366) interprets 24 hours a day.

Questions people ask

How do I find something I lost in Korea?

Check with the place or transport operator first (station staff, taxi company, airport), then search Lost112, the police's national lost and found site, which has an English section. Unclaimed items reach the police after about a week.

Where is the Seoul subway lost and found?

Seoul Metro has lost and found centres at City Hall, Chungmuro, Wangsimni and Taereung Entrance stations, open weekdays 9am–6pm. Call 1577-1234 for help.

How do I find a phone I left in a Korean taxi?

If you booked through an app, the driver's contact is in your ride history. If you paid by card, call T-money (1644-1188) to trace the taxi. A receipt shows the plate number and company phone.

What do I do if I lose my passport in Korea?

Report it at a police station and get a loss report, then contact your embassy for an emergency passport or travel document. Call 1345 (immigration) to check what you need before departure.

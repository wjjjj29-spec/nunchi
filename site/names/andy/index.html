Andy in Korean 앤디

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Andy as 앤디: two syllables, said aen-di. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Pronunciation tip

ㅐ (ae) is said like the e in "bed" by most Koreans today, a little more closed than the a in "cat".

  • Two even beats, aen-di: Korean doesn't stress one syllable over the other the way English does.

How Koreans would call you

Friends calling you over
앤디야!
앤디 ends in a vowel, so it takes 야. Casual, for close friends.
At work or with new people
앤디 씨
앤디 ssi. Polite, roughly "Mr" or "Ms", but used with the first name.
Customer, client, online
앤디 님
앤디 nim. What you'll see on a delivery box or a booking.

Korean given names are almost always two syllables, so 앤디 already sounds natural to Korean ears. With foreign names some Koreans skip 야 and just say 앤디 or 앤디 씨.

Why it's spelled 앤디

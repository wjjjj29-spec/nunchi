Korean pharmacy guide: what to ask for, in Korean

Headache, cold, stomach, motion sickness: the words to say, the brands every Korean knows, and where to go when the pharmacy is shut.

3 min readChecked 18 October 2026
At a glance1 / 6
The short version
  • No prescription needed for everyday medicine: cold and flu, fever and pain, indigestion, diarrhoea, allergies and patches are all sold over the counter at a pharmacy (약국).
  • Say the symptom: 두통 headache, 열이 나요 fever, 목이 아파요 sore throat, 배탈 upset stomach. Or show the Korean on your phone.
  • After hours: 24-hour convenience stores sell a short list of basics. On Sundays, search 휴일지킴이약국 for the duty pharmacy.
  • Own medicine: up to six bottles for personal use. Narcotic or psychotropic medicine needs approval before you arrive.

How Korean pharmacies work

Look for the green cross and the word 약국 (yakguk). Pharmacies are everywhere, often right below a clinic, and the pharmacist will hand you medicine from behind the counter after you describe what's wrong. Everyday medicine is sold without a prescription. Antibiotics, sleeping pills and the contraceptive pill are prescription-only, so for those you need to see a doctor first.

Most pharmacies open around 9am to 8 or 9pm on weekdays, close earlier on Saturdays and shut on Sundays. Pharmacists' English varies. It's good in Itaewon, Gangnam and near big hospitals, patchy elsewhere, so have the Korean ready.

Nunchi tip

Hangover drinks like Condition 컨디션 aren't medicine in Korea, they're drinks, so you'll find them in the convenience store fridge, not at the pharmacy. Koreans drink them before the night out, not after.

When the pharmacy is closed

  • 24-hour convenience stores (CU, GS25, 7-Eleven, Emart24) sell a short approved list: Tylenol, cold medicine like Pancol A and Panpyrin T, digestives like Festal and Bearse, and pain patches. Each pack is about one day's dose, and they won't sell to under-12s.
  • Sundays and holidays: each area has duty pharmacies. Search 휴일지킴이약국 on pharm114.or.kr, or open hospitals and pharmacies on E-Gen. Listings can be out of date on holidays, so call ahead if you can.
  • Late night: some cities run public late-night pharmacies (공공심야약국) until about 1am.

Bringing your own medicine

Korean customs generally allows up to six bottles of medicine for personal use. Keep it in the original packaging with your prescription or a doctor's letter. Narcotic and psychotropic medicine is different: it needs approval from the Ministry of Food and Drug Safety (MFDS) before you arrive, whatever your nationality. That includes many ADHD medicines. Amphetamine-based ones such as Adderall are treated as narcotics in Korea, so check with MFDS or a Korean embassy well before you fly.

Questions people ask

Can tourists buy medicine in Korea without a prescription?

Yes. Cold and flu medicine, painkillers, digestives, antidiarrhoeals, allergy tablets and pain patches are all sold over the counter at pharmacies. Antibiotics and sleeping pills need a prescription.

What is Korean cold medicine called?

Ask for 감기약 (gamgiyak). Popular brands are Pancol A (판콜에이), a small liquid bottle, and Panpyrin T (판피린티). Both are also sold in 24-hour convenience stores.

Are pharmacies open on Sunday in Korea?

Most are closed, but each area has duty pharmacies. Search 휴일지킴이약국 on pharm114.or.kr or use E-Gen. Convenience stores sell basic medicine 24 hours.

Can I bring my own medicine to Korea?

Usually up to six bottles for personal use, in original packaging with a prescription. Narcotic and psychotropic medicine, including many ADHD drugs, needs MFDS approval before you travel.

Freddie in Korean 프레디

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Freddie as 프레디: three syllables, said peu-re-di. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Pronunciation tip

ㄹ between two vowels (프레) is one quick tap of the tongue, like the tt in American "butter": closer to a soft d than an English r.

  • 프 carries the filler vowel ㅡ (eu). Keep it short and light, almost just the consonant.
  • ㅍ at the start is aspirated: a clear puff of air, like the p in "pie".
  • Give all 3 syllables the same weight. Korean has no strong stress, so it's peu-re-di, evenly.

How Koreans would call you

Friends calling you over

프레디 ends in a vowel, so it takes 야. Casual, for close friends.
At work or with new people
프레디 씨
프레디 ssi. Polite, roughly "Mr" or "Ms", but used with the first name.
Customer, client, online
프레디 님
프레디 nim. What you'll see on a delivery box or a booking.

Korean given names are almost always two syllables (after a one-syllable family name), so at 3 syllables 프레디 reads as a foreign name at a glance. With foreign names some Koreans skip 야 and just say 프레디 or 프레디 씨.

Why it's spelled 프레디

Here the letters and the sound line up: applying the loanword rules letter by letter already gives 프레디, the same spelling Korean media use.

  • Korean has no f sound, so f becomes ㅍ (p), said with a puff of air.
  • Double letters count once. Hangul spells the sound, not the letters.
  • Korean adds the vowel ㅡ to consonants that have no vowel of their own (프), which is why Freddie has 3 syllables in Korean.

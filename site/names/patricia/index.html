Patricia in Korean 퍼트리샤

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Patricia as 퍼트리샤: four syllables, said peo-teu-ri-sya. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Pronunciation tip

ㄹ between two vowels (트리) is one quick tap of the tongue, like the tt in American "butter": closer to a soft d than an English r.

  • 트 carries the filler vowel ㅡ (eu). Keep it short and light, almost just the consonant.
  • ㅓ (eo) in 퍼 is the open "uh" of "sun", not an "o".
  • ㅍ at the start is aspirated: a clear puff of air, like the p in "pie".
  • Give all 4 syllables the same weight. Korean has no strong stress, so it's peo-teu-ri-sya, evenly.

Korean given names are almost always two syllables (after a one-syllable family name), so at 4 syllables 퍼트리샤 reads as a foreign name at a glance. With foreign names some Koreans skip 야 and just say 퍼트리샤 or 퍼트리샤 씨.

How Koreans write it

Spelled letter-by-letter you'd get 파트리시아, but Korean media write 퍼트리샤, because the name is written the way it sounds, not the way it's spelled.

  • The unstressed "uh" vowel is written ㅓ (eo), whatever letter English uses for it.
  • Korean adds the vowel ㅡ to consonants that have no vowel of their own (트), which is why Patricia has 4 syllables in Korean.

How do you pronounce Patricia in Korean?

Say it peo-teu-ri-sya, with even weight on every syllable. ㄹ between two vowels (트리) is one quick tap of the tongue, like the tt in American "butter": closer to a soft d than an English r.

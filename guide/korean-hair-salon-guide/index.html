Getting your hair done in Korea: booking, prices and what to say

Cut, perm, colour or nails: how to book without a Korean number, which designer to pick, what it costs in 2026 and the Korean to show.

Shampoo is usually included. There's no tipping.

Booking: Naver, Kakao Hair Shop or walk in

  • Naver Map. Most salons take bookings through Naver. Search the salon, tap 예약 (yeyak, booking), then pick the service, the designer and a time. Since 4 June 2026, Naver lets foreigners verify with an overseas passport on their phone and book and pay without a Korean phone number. Older guides say you need a Korean number; that's out of date, although each salon still decides what it offers online.
  • Kakao Hair Shop (카카오헤어샵) is the other booking app. It shows styles and prices before you book and you normally pay upfront in the app. Some salons would rather you book through Naver, because Kakao charges them a commission.
  • English platforms. Creatrip and Trazy list foreigner-friendly salons with prices in English and online payment. You can't always choose your designer this way.
  • Walk-ins. Neighbourhood salons and barbershops often take walk-ins on weekdays. At popular salons, a weekend walk-in is close to impossible. For a quick men's cut, budget chains such as Blue Club charge about ₩10,000–12,000.

Designer levels: 원장, 실장, 디자이너

Korean salons price by who holds the scissors. On Naver you'll see designers listed by title:

  • 원장 (wonjang, director): often the owner or the most senior designer, and the most expensive.
  • 실장 (siljang, senior designer or manager): in between.
  • 디자이너 (dijaineo, designer): the most affordable.

Some chains use grades instead. Soonsiki Hair in Hongdae ranks designers P, S, D and M by years of experience, and charges a flat ₩33,000 for a cut if you don't pick one. The gap can be large: a Korean shopper quoted in 2025 was charged ₩60,000 for a director's "design cut". If price matters more than a name, ask for a designer rather than the 원장.

Length surcharges, add-ons and the price rule

The posted price is often the starting price. Most salons add a length surcharge (기장 추가, gijang chuga) for perms and colour, sometimes with tiers as fine as jaw, shoulder and collarbone length, and it can cost more than the service itself. Designers also suggest add-ons such as a nourishing treatment (영양제, yeongyangje). It's fine to say 괜찮아요 (gwaenchanayo, "I'm fine, thanks").

The law helps. If you're having three or more services, such as a cut, perm and colour, the salon must show you the final price of each and the total before it starts. Salons of 66㎡ or more must also post their final prices outside. Before anything starts, ask 총 얼마예요? (chong eolmayeyo?, "How much is it in total?").

A photo works better than any phrase. Koreans bring screenshots of a celebrity or a style they like, and designers expect it. Because many Koreans have straight hair, a standard perm here gives soft waves. Ask for a straight perm (매직, maejik) if you want the opposite.

Nunchi tip

If your designer asks 이따 약속 있으세요? ("Any plans after this?"), say yes if you want the full blow-dry and styling. Say no and you may leave with it simply dried.

Shampoo, tipping and paying

  • Shampoo: usually part of a cut at trend salons, along with a blow-dry. Some neighbourhood salons charge for the shampoo separately, so check if the price looks low.
  • Tipping: none. It isn't part of Korean service culture, and nobody will expect it.
  • Paying: at the counter when you finish, unless you prepaid online. Cards are accepted almost everywhere; see our cash and cards guide. You'll often be offered a prepaid membership with a discount, which only makes sense if you live here.
  • Cancelling: give at least 24 hours' notice. Booked salons may charge for late cancellations or no-shows.

Questions people ask

How much is a haircut in Seoul?

In June 2026 the Seoul average was ₩24,462 for a women's cut and ₩13,231 for a men's cut, according to the Korea Consumer Agency. Trend salons in Hongdae and Gangnam charge about ₩30,000–60,000, more with a director (원장).

Can foreigners book a Korean hair salon on Naver?

Yes. Since 4 June 2026, Naver lets foreigners verify with an overseas passport and book and pay without a Korean phone number. Search the salon on Naver Map and tap 예약 (booking).

What is the difference between 원장, 실장 and 디자이너?

They're designer titles. 원장 (wonjang) is the director, often the owner, and the most expensive. 실장 (siljang) is a senior designer. 디자이너 (dijaineo) is a designer and the most affordable.

Do you tip at hair salons in Korea?

No. Tipping isn't part of Korean service culture. Shampoo and a blow-dry are usually included in a cut, though some neighbourhood salons charge for the shampoo.

Why was my perm more expensive than the listed price?

Most salons add a length surcharge (기장 추가) for perms and colour, and designers often suggest treatments. For three or more services the salon must show you each final price and the total first, so ask 총 얼마예요?

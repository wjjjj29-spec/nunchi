Cash, cards and ATMs in Korea: what tourists need in 2026

Your card works almost everywhere, until it doesn't. Where you still need won, which ATMs take foreign cards, and the prepaid cards that skip the hassle.

3 min readChecked 18 October 2026
At a glance1 / 6
The short version
  • Card first: foreign Visa and Mastercard work in most shops, cafés, restaurants and taxis. Always choose to pay in KRW.
  • Some cash: for traditional markets, street food and topping up T-money, which is mostly cash only.
  • ATMs: use machines marked "Global ATM" and expect about ₩3,500–5,000 per withdrawal, plus your bank's fee.
  • Easiest: a prepaid tourist card (WOWPASS, NAMANE) or Wise/Revolut. No tipping anywhere.

Cards: yes, almost everywhere

Korea is one of the most card-friendly countries in the world. Foreign Visa and Mastercard work in convenience stores, chains, cafés, most restaurants and taxis. Problems show up at the edges: a small shop's reader fails, or a merchant doesn't accept foreign cards because of the fees. Online sites that need Korean identity verification won't take them either.

  • Pay in KRW. If the terminal offers to charge you in your home currency, say no. That "convenience" usually adds 3–8%.
  • Apple Pay: only a small share of terminals are NFC, so it works at some chains, convenience stores and department stores but not reliably. Carry your physical card.
  • Taxis all have card readers. Book through k.ride or Uber to pay by foreign card in the app.

Where you still need cash

  • Traditional markets and street food: Gwangjang, Namdaemun and most stalls prefer cash.
  • T-money top-ups: station machines and convenience-store counters mostly take cash only. Since March 2026, the new kiosks at Seoul subway Lines 1–8 accept foreign cards, but for buying Climate Card passes, with a fee of about 3.7%.
  • Buses: many Seoul buses no longer take cash at all, so tap a T-money card instead.

A few ₩10,000 and ₩5,000 notes for market days are plenty for most trips.

ATMs: look for "Global ATM"

Not every Korean ATM takes foreign cards. Look for a Global ATM sign. Most major banks (Woori, Hana, KB, Shinhan) have them, and so do airports. Expect a local fee of roughly ₩3,500–5,000 per withdrawal, plus whatever your home bank charges. Convenience-store ATMs are open late but usually cost more. If the screen asks whether to convert, choose KRW.

Exchanging money

Change only a little at the airport. Rates there are noticeably worse. In Seoul, the licensed money changers in Myeongdong usually give the best rates for cash, and banks are fine too. Or skip exchange altogether with one of the cards below.

Nunchi tip

iPhone users can now add T-money to Apple Wallet: open the Tmoney app, tap "Foreigner" and top up with Mastercard or Amex (not Visa). Then just tap your phone at the gate.

Tipping and tax

There's no tipping in Korea, and staff may hand it back. Some upscale hotels and restaurants add a 10% service charge, shown on the bill as 봉사료 (bongsaryo). Prices on tags already include tax.

Shopping? At stores marked Tax Free, purchases of ₩15,000 or more can get part of the VAT back, often instantly at the till with your passport.

Questions people ask

Do I need cash in Korea?

A little. Cards work in most places, but traditional markets, street food and T-money top-ups at machines mostly need cash. A few ₩10,000 notes cover most trips.

Which ATMs accept foreign cards in Korea?

Look for ATMs marked Global ATM, found at major banks such as Woori, Hana, KB and Shinhan, and at airports. Expect about ₩3,500–5,000 per withdrawal plus your bank's fee.

Does Apple Pay work in Korea for tourists?

Only at NFC terminals, which are a minority. It works at some chains and convenience stores. Carry a physical card. iPhone users can add T-money to Apple Wallet through the Tmoney app.

Do you tip in Korea?

No. Tipping isn't customary and prices include tax. Some upscale hotels add a 10% service charge, labelled 봉사료 on the bill.

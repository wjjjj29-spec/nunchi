The clock is the famous trap: 3:30 is 세 시 삼십 분, native for the hour and Sino for the minutes, in the same breath. Every learner says 삼 시 at least once. Everyone will understand you. Nobody will correct you, which is why you should get it right yourself.

Why Korean counts in ten-thousands

English groups digits in threes: thousand, million, billion. Korean groups them in fours: 만 (10,000), 억 (100,000,000), 조 (a trillion). So 1,000,000 is not "a thousand thousands" but 백만, a hundred ten-thousands. When you look at a Korean price tag, read it from the 만: 350,000원 is 삼십오만 원, "thirty-five man won". Once that clicks, rent and salary conversations get much easier.

Questions people ask

Why does Korean have two number systems?

Native Korean numbers are the original counting words. Sino-Korean numbers arrived with Chinese characters and took over anything written or mathematical. Native numbers stop at 99, so big numbers are always Sino-Korean.

How do you say 10,000 in Korean?

만 (man). Not 십천. Korean has no word for "ten thousand" built from thousand; 만 is its own unit and the base for everything larger.

Do the native numbers change before a counter?

Yes, four of them shorten: 하나→한, 둘→두, 셋→세, 넷→네 when a counter follows. One coffee is 커피 한 잔, not 하나 잔. Twenty also shortens: 스물→스무 (스무 살, twenty years old).

What is 영 vs 공 for zero?

영 is zero in maths and amounts. 공 is zero when reading digits one by one, like phone numbers: 010 is 공일공.

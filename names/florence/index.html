Florence in Korean 플로렌스

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Florence as 플로렌스: four syllables, said peul-lo-ren-seu. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Pronunciation tip

ㄹ between two vowels (로렌) is one quick tap of the tongue, like the tt in American "butter": closer to a soft d than an English r.

  • The double ㄹ in 플로 is a clear English l, as in "hello", not an r.
  • 플 and 스 carry the filler vowel ㅡ (eu). Keep them short and light, almost just the consonant.
  • ㅍ at the start is aspirated: a clear puff of air, like the p in "pie".
  • ㅗ is a pure "o" with rounded lips. Don't add the w-glide English puts in "go".

Korean given names are almost always two syllables (after a one-syllable family name), so at 4 syllables 플로렌스 reads as a foreign name at a glance. With foreign names some Koreans skip 야 and just say 플로렌스 or 플로렌스 씨.

Why it's spelled 플로렌스

How Koreans write it

Spelled letter-by-letter you'd get 프로렌세, but Korean media write 플로렌스, because the name is written the way it sounds, not the way it's spelled.

  • Korean has no f sound, so f becomes ㅍ (p), said with a puff of air.
  • An l between vowels is written twice (final ㄹ + ㄹ), so Koreans say a clear l instead of a tapped r.
  • Korean adds the vowel ㅡ to consonants that have no vowel of their own (플 and 스), which is why Florence has 4 syllables in Korean.

Questions people ask

How do you write Florence in Korean?

Florence is written 플로렌스 in Hangul (Revised Romanization: peullorenseu). It is the standard spelling Korean media use for the name. In Hangul it has four syllables: 플 + 로 + 렌 + 스.

How do you pronounce Florence in Korean?

Say it peul-lo-ren-seu, with even weight on every syllable. ㄹ between two vowels (로렌) is one quick tap of the tongue, like the tt in American "butter": closer to a soft d than an English r.

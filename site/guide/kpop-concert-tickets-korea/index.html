How to get K-pop concert tickets in Korea as a foreigner

Use the global ticket sites, verify your passport before the sale, join the fan club in time and don't touch resale. Here's how it works in 2026.

4 min readChecked 29 October 2026
At a glance1 / 6
The short version
  • Where: buy on the global sites, NOL World (formerly Interpark Global), Melon Ticket Global, YES24 Global or Ticketlink Global. The Korean pages need Korean phone verification.
  • Fan club: many of the best seats go in the presale. Buy the global membership, verify it on the ticket site in the window, then buy in your slot, usually from 8pm KST.
  • ID: verify your passport (and face, on NOL World) before the sale, use your exact passport name and book on your own account. Limits are often one or two tickets.
  • Resale: illegal above face value since 28 August 2026, and tickets are ID-checked at entry, so skip resellers.

Use the global site, not the Korean one

Every big Korean ticket site has two faces. The Korean pages need Korean identity verification (본인인증 bonin injeung, identity check), done with a Korean mobile number in your own name, which tourists can't get. The global pages let you sign up with an email address and pay with an overseas card. Each concert notice names which site sells the show and whether there's a global page. If a show has no global page, there's generally no legitimate way to buy it as a tourist.

Fan-club presales come first

For idol concerts, a big share of the good seats goes in the fan-club presale, before general sale. Getting in takes three steps, each with its own deadline:

  • Buy the global membership. Notices name it, for example "(GLOBAL)" or "(GL)". It has to stay active until the concert, or your presale ticket can be cancelled.
  • Verify it on the ticket site in the verification window, usually a few days before the presale. Miss it and the membership doesn't count.
  • Buy in your presale slot. Global and Korean presales are often on different days. For Yesung's 2026 Seoul show on Ticketlink, the global presale was the day after the Korean one.

Sales usually open at 8pm Korea time (KST, UTC+9). Work out what that is where you live before the day.

Passport checks and ticket limits

  • Verify before the sale. On NOL World you photograph your passport and your face on your phone from the concert page. Do it days ahead: if it's declined you have to ask for a reset and wait for an administrator.
  • Names must match. Enter your name exactly as on your passport. NOL World's notices say entry is only granted when the booking and the attendee's ID match perfectly.
  • Everyone books for themselves. NOL World says each ticket must be booked on the attendee's own account, including children under 14. Buying for a friend doesn't work.
  • Limits are low. Each show sets its own. TREASURE's June 2026 Seoul shows allowed one ticket per person, per show, per platform. Yesung's allowed one in presale and two in total.

Beating the queue

  • One device, one queue. NOL World gives one queue number per account, and if you join from several devices or browsers at once, your existing place is closed. Ignore old advice to open five tabs.
  • Know your timers. On NOL World you get up to 10 minutes to pick seats, then 7 minutes to pay before they're released.
  • Have the card ready. Turn on overseas online payments with your bank before the day and keep a second card handy. More in our cards and cash guide.
  • Budget for fees. NOL World adds a service fee of ₩8,000 per concert ticket on top of the face price. TXT's November 2026 KSPO Dome shows were ₩165,000–253,000.
  • Try again later. Unpaid bank-transfer bookings are cancelled after the deadline and those seats come back. Check again the next day and in the week before the show.

Collecting your ticket

For most big NOL World concerts there's no paper ticket and no will-call: you get a mobile ticket. On the day you either use Face Pass (register your face in advance and walk through) or go to the IN-PASS booth, show your passport and get a wristband. Other shows still use card tickets, mailed or collected on site. Whatever the notice says, bring your physical passport, not a photo or copy, and arrive early, as ID checks take time. Venue data can be slow, so open your mobile ticket before you get there. If you need data at all, get it sorted first with the eSIM data calculator.

Resale: don't

Since 28 August 2026, Korea's amended Performance Act bans habitual or commercial resale above face value without the seller's consent, whether or not a bot was used. Fines go up to 50 times the sale price, on top of possible prison time. Tickets are tied to the buyer's name and passport, so a resold ticket probably won't get you through the gate either. Avoid sellers on X, Instagram and Telegram, and treat anyone asking for gift cards or crypto as a scam.

Questions people ask

Can foreigners buy K-pop concert tickets in Korea?

Yes, on the global versions of the ticket sites: NOL World (formerly Interpark Global), Melon Ticket Global, YES24 Global and Ticketlink Global. The Korean pages need Korean phone verification. The concert notice says which site sells the show.

What happened to Interpark Global?

It was renamed NOL World in December 2025 and now lives at world.nol.com. The Korean site is NOL Ticket.

Do I need a passport to buy K-pop tickets in Korea?

For most big shows, yes. NOL World asks you to verify your passport and face before booking, and on the day your ID must match the booking name exactly. Bring the physical passport.

How many tickets can I buy?

It's set per show and is usually low for idol concerts. Recent examples allowed one ticket per person per show, or one in presale and two in total.

Is it legal to buy resale K-pop tickets in Korea?

Since 28 August 2026, habitual or commercial resale above face value is banned, with fines up to 50 times the sale price. Tickets are ID-checked, so resold tickets often fail at the gate.

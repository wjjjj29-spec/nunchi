Ryan in Korean 라이언

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Ryan as 라이언: three syllables, said ra-i-eon. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Pronunciation tip

The opening ㄹ is a single light tap, halfway between an English r and l. Never roll it.

  • ㅓ (eo) in 언 is the open "uh" of "sun", not an "o".
  • ㅇ at the top of 이 is silent, so 이 is just the vowel: i.
  • Give all 3 syllables the same weight. Korean has no strong stress, so it's ra-i-eon, evenly.

라이언 nim. What you'll see on a delivery box or a booking.

Korean given names are almost always two syllables (after a one-syllable family name), so at 3 syllables 라이언 reads as a foreign name at a glance. In casual sentences Koreans often add 이 after a name that ends in a consonant: 라이언이 왔어 ("Ryan's here").

How Koreans write it

Spelled letter-by-letter you'd get 르얀, but Korean media write 라이언, because the name is written the way it sounds, not the way it's spelled.

  • The long i (as in "my") is spelled 아이: two beats in Korean, one in English.
  • The unstressed "uh" vowel is written ㅓ (eo), whatever letter English uses for it.

Questions people ask

How do you write Ryan in Korean?

How do you pronounce Ryan in Korean?

Say it ra-i-eon, with even weight on every syllable. The opening ㄹ is a single light tap, halfway between an English r and l. Never roll it.

Korea travel apps: what to download before you fly

Google Maps won't walk you anywhere, and half the country runs on apps you've never heard of. These are the ones that work for visitors in 2026.

3 min readChecked 13 October 2026
At a glance1 / 6
The short version
  • Maps: Naver Map. Google Maps still has no turn-by-turn directions in Korea.
  • Taxis: k.ride (Kakao's app for visitors) or Uber. Both take foreign cards.
  • Food: Baemin now works in English, with foreign cards and no Korean number. CatchTable Global for restaurant waitlists.
  • Also useful: Papago (translation), Korail or Klook (trains), WOWPASS (cash card plus T-money), KakaoTalk (messages).

Maps: use Naver Map

Google Maps is fine for finding places, reading reviews and checking bus and subway routes. It still can't give turn-by-turn walking or driving directions in Korea. Korea only approved exporting detailed map data in February 2026, and Google hasn't switched navigation on yet.

Naver Map fills that gap. It runs in English and translates reviews, and it's the map most visitors use. Since June 2026 you can also verify with your passport to book and pay inside it. KakaoMap is good too, but more of it stays in Korean.

Nunchi tip

If an English search finds nothing, paste the place's Korean name or its phone number. Both match far more reliably than romanised names.

Taxis: k.ride, Uber or Kakao T

  • k.ride is Kakao Mobility's app for visitors. It's in English, you sign in with email, Google or Apple, and you pay with a foreign card added in advance. It also translates your chat with the driver. Fares include a small extra fee.
  • Uber dispatches licensed taxis with your existing account. Plenty in Seoul, Busan and Incheon, fewer elsewhere.
  • Kakao T is what Koreans use and has the most cars. It accepts foreign cards for regular taxis, though some foreign cards are still declined.

Food delivery: Baemin finally works

Ordering delivery used to be hard without a Korean phone number. That changed in 2026. Baemin (Baedal Minjok), Korea's biggest delivery app, added English menus in February. Since July you can check out without a Korean number, paying with a foreign card, Apple Pay or Alipay+.

Coupang Eats and Yogiyo still work best with a Korean number and card. To order to a hotel, copy its exact Korean address from Naver Map and add your room number.

Restaurants: CatchTable for the queue

Popular restaurants run digital waitlists instead of reservations. CatchTable Global is the English version: search in 20 languages, join a waitlist remotely and pay a deposit by card, with no Korean number. You'll also see Tabling tablets at restaurant doors; they're Korean-only, so ask staff to add you.

Trains: Korail

KTX tickets are easiest on the Korail English website or through Klook, which has sold Korail tickets since April 2026. Korea's own Korail app was renamed Korail+ in August 2026 and now books SRT trains too, but it's built for Korean users. Book early for Lunar New Year and Chuseok, when trains sell out weeks ahead.

Paying and getting around

  • WOWPASS: a prepaid card for visitors. Exchange cash at its machines (airport and major stations, passport needed), then pay in shops and top up the built-in T-money for transit.
  • Subway tickets: since March 2026, ticket machines at Seoul subway Lines 1–8 accept foreign Visa and Mastercard, with a fee of about 3.7%.
  • Kakao Pay and Toss aren't built for tourists. Kakao Pay still only takes Korean-issued cards.

Talking: Papago and KakaoTalk

Papago translates text, voice and photos. Point the camera at a menu or a sign. Download the offline language pack before you fly. KakaoTalk is how guesthouses, tour guides and Korean friends will message you. Sign up with your home number.

If something goes wrong

Call 1330, the Korea Travel Hotline, for free interpretation (also in the VisitKorea app). The government's Emergency Ready App sends disaster alerts in 22 languages. Emergency numbers: 112 police, 119 fire and ambulance.

Questions people ask

Does Google Maps work in Korea in 2026?

Partly. It shows places, reviews and public transport routes, but still no turn-by-turn walking or driving directions. Use Naver Map for getting around.

Can tourists order food delivery in Korea?

Yes. Baemin has English menus and, since July 2026, no longer needs a Korean phone number. It takes foreign cards, Apple Pay and Alipay+.

What taxi app should tourists use in Korea?

k.ride (Kakao's app for visitors) or Uber. Both work in English with a foreign card. Kakao T has the most cars and takes foreign cards for regular taxis.

Do I need a Korean phone number for apps in Korea?

Much less than before. Naver Map, k.ride, Uber, Baemin, CatchTable Global and KakaoTalk all work with a foreign number or none. Banking and Kakao Pay still need Korean ID.

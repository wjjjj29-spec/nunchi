Michael in Korean 마이클

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Michael as 마이클: three syllables, said ma-i-keul. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Pronunciation tip

클 carries the filler vowel ㅡ (eu). Keep it short and light, almost just the consonant.

  • ㄹ at the bottom of 클 is an l: the tongue touches just behind the teeth and stays there.
  • ㅇ at the top of 이 is silent, so 이 is just the vowel: i.
  • Give all 3 syllables the same weight. Korean has no strong stress, so it's ma-i-keul, evenly.

Korean given names are almost always two syllables (after a one-syllable family name), so at 3 syllables 마이클 reads as a foreign name at a glance. In casual sentences Koreans often add 이 after a name that ends in a consonant: 마이클이 왔어 ("Michael's here").

Why it's spelled 마이클

How Koreans write it
마이클
the settled media spelling

Spelled letter-by-letter you'd get 미차엘, but Korean media write 마이클, because the name is written the way it sounds, not the way it's spelled.

  • The ch in Michael is a k sound, so Korean uses ㅋ (k), not ㅊ.
  • The long i (as in "my") is spelled 아이: two beats in Korean, one in English.
  • Korean adds the vowel ㅡ to consonants that have no vowel of their own (클), which is why Michael has 3 syllables in Korean.

How do you pronounce Michael in Korean?

Say it ma-i-keul, with even weight on every syllable. 클 carries the filler vowel ㅡ (eu). Keep it short and light, almost just the consonant.

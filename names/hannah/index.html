Hannah in Korean 해나

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Hannah as 해나: two syllables, said hae-na. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Hannah in Hangul
해나
Revised Romanization: haena · said hae-na · two syllables

Hannah letter by letter

Each Hangul block is one syllable, built from a consonant, a vowel and sometimes a final consonant underneath.

Pronunciation tip

ㅐ (ae) is said like the e in "bed" by most Koreans today, a little more closed than the a in "cat".

  • Two even beats, hae-na: Korean doesn't stress one syllable over the other the way English does.

How Koreans would call you

Friends calling you over
해나야!
해나 ends in a vowel, so it takes 야. Casual, for close friends.
At work or with new people
해나 씨
해나 ssi. Polite, roughly "Mr" or "Ms", but used with the first name.
Customer, client, online
해나 님
해나 nim. What you'll see on a delivery box or a booking.

Korean given names are almost always two syllables, so 해나 already sounds natural to Korean ears. With foreign names some Koreans skip 야 and just say 해나 or 해나 씨.

Why it's spelled 해나

Here the letters and the sound line up: applying the loanword rules letter by letter already gives 해나, the same spelling Korean media use.

  • The final h is silent and is dropped.
  • Double letters count once. Hangul spells the sound, not the letters.

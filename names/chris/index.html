Chris in Korean 크리스

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Chris as 크리스: three syllables, said keu-ri-seu. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Pronunciation tip

ㄹ between two vowels (크리) is one quick tap of the tongue, like the tt in American "butter": closer to a soft d than an English r.

  • 크 and 스 carry the filler vowel ㅡ (eu). Keep them short and light, almost just the consonant.
  • ㅋ at the start is aspirated: a clear puff of air, like the k in "kite".
  • Give all 3 syllables the same weight. Korean has no strong stress, so it's keu-ri-seu, evenly.

How Koreans would call you

Friends calling you over
크리스야!
크리스 ends in a vowel, so it takes 야. Casual, for close friends.
At work or with new people
크리스 씨
크리스 ssi. Polite, roughly "Mr" or "Ms", but used with the first name.
Customer, client, online
크리스 님
크리스 nim. What you'll see on a delivery box or a booking.

Korean given names are almost always two syllables (after a one-syllable family name), so at 3 syllables 크리스 reads as a foreign name at a glance. With foreign names some Koreans skip 야 and just say 크리스 or 크리스 씨.

Why it's spelled 크리스

Here the letters and the sound line up: applying the loanword rules letter by letter already gives 크리스, the same spelling Korean media use.

  • The ch in Chris is a k sound, so Korean uses ㅋ (k), not ㅊ.
  • Korean adds the vowel ㅡ to consonants that have no vowel of their own (크 and 스), which is why Chris has 3 syllables in Korean.

Miguel in Korean 미겔

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Miguel as 미겔: two syllables, said mi-gel. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Miguel letter by letter

Each Hangul block is one syllable, built from a consonant, a vowel and sometimes a final consonant underneath.

Pronunciation tip

ㄹ at the bottom of 겔 is an l: the tongue touches just behind the teeth and stays there.

  • Two even beats, mi-gel: Korean doesn't stress one syllable over the other the way English does.

미겔 nim. What you'll see on a delivery box or a booking.

Korean given names are almost always two syllables, so 미겔 already sounds natural to Korean ears. In casual sentences Koreans often add 이 after a name that ends in a consonant: 미겔이 왔어 ("Miguel's here").

Spelled letter-by-letter you'd get 미굴, but Korean media write 미겔, because the name is written the way it sounds in Spanish, not the way it's spelled.

  • Korean writes Spanish names by their Spanish sound: vowels are read as written.

Questions people ask

How do you write Miguel in Korean?

Miguel is written 미겔 in Hangul (Revised Romanization: migel). It is the standard spelling Korean media use for the name and follows the Spanish pronunciation. In Hangul it has two syllables: 미 + 겔.

How do you pronounce Miguel in Korean?

Say it mi-gel, with even weight on every syllable. ㄹ at the bottom of 겔 is an l: the tongue touches just behind the teeth and stays there.

Debra in Korean 데브라

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Debra as 데브라: three syllables, said de-beu-ra. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Pronunciation tip

ㄹ between two vowels (브라) is one quick tap of the tongue, like the tt in American "butter": closer to a soft d than an English r.

  • 브 carries the filler vowel ㅡ (eu). Keep it short and light, almost just the consonant.
  • ㄷ at the start is soft and unvoiced, halfway between t and d.
  • Give all 3 syllables the same weight. Korean has no strong stress, so it's de-beu-ra, evenly.

How Koreans would call you

Friends calling you over
데브라야!
데브라 ends in a vowel, so it takes 야. Casual, for close friends.
At work or with new people
데브라 씨
데브라 ssi. Polite, roughly "Mr" or "Ms", but used with the first name.
Customer, client, online
데브라 님
데브라 nim. What you'll see on a delivery box or a booking.

Korean given names are almost always two syllables (after a one-syllable family name), so at 3 syllables 데브라 reads as a foreign name at a glance. With foreign names some Koreans skip 야 and just say 데브라 or 데브라 씨.

Why it's spelled 데브라

Here the letters and the sound line up: applying the loanword rules letter by letter already gives 데브라, the same spelling Korean media use.

  • Korean adds the vowel ㅡ to consonants that have no vowel of their own (브), which is why Debra has 3 syllables in Korean.

How do you pronounce Debra in Korean?

Say it de-beu-ra, with even weight on every syllable. ㄹ between two vowels (브라) is one quick tap of the tongue, like the tt in American "butter": closer to a soft d than an English r.

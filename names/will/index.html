Will in Korean 윌

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Will as 윌: one syllable, said wil. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Will in Hangul
윌
Revised Romanization: wil · said wil · one syllable

Will letter by letter

Each Hangul block is one syllable, built from a consonant, a vowel and sometimes a final consonant underneath.

윌
ㅇ silent + ㅟ wi + ㄹ l (final)
wil
Pronunciation tip

ㄹ at the bottom of 윌 is an l: the tongue touches just behind the teeth and stays there.

  • ㅟ in 윌 is one gliding vowel: wee, in a single beat.
  • It's a single syllable in Korean, so say it in one short beat: wil.

How Koreans would call you

Friends calling you over
윌아!
윌 ends in a consonant, so it takes 아, said 위라. Casual, for close friends.
At work or with new people
윌 씨
윌 ssi. Polite, roughly "Mr" or "Ms", but used with the first name.
Customer, client, online
윌 님
윌 nim. What you'll see on a delivery box or a booking.

One syllable is short for Korea, where given names are almost always two, so 윌 stands out and is easy to remember. In casual sentences Koreans often add 이 after a name that ends in a consonant: 윌이 왔어 ("Will's here").

Why it's spelled 윌

Here the letters and the sound line up: applying the loanword rules letter by letter already gives 윌, the same spelling Korean media use.

  • w merges with the vowel after it into one Korean vowel: 윌.

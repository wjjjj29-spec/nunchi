Vijay in Korean 비자이

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Vijay as 비자이: three syllables, said bi-ja-i. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Pronunciation tip

ㅂ at the start is soft and unvoiced, halfway between p and b.

  • ㅇ at the top of 이 is silent, so 이 is just the vowel: i.
  • Give all 3 syllables the same weight. Korean has no strong stress, so it's bi-ja-i, evenly.

How Koreans would call you

At work or with new people
비자이 씨
비자이 ssi. Polite, roughly "Mr" or "Ms", but used with the first name.
Customer, client, online
비자이 님
비자이 nim. What you'll see on a delivery box or a booking.

Korean given names are almost always two syllables (after a one-syllable family name), so at 3 syllables 비자이 reads as a foreign name at a glance. With foreign names some Koreans skip 야 and just say 비자이 or 비자이 씨.

How Koreans write it
비자이
the settled media spelling

Spelled letter-by-letter you'd get 비제이, but Korean media write 비자이, because the name is written the way it sounds, not the way it's spelled.

  • There is no v in Korean either: v becomes ㅂ (b).
  • The long i (as in "my") is spelled 아이: two beats in Korean, one in English.

Questions people ask

How do you write Vijay in Korean?

Vijay is written 비자이 in Hangul (Revised Romanization: bijai). It is the standard spelling Korean media use for the name. In Hangul it has three syllables: 비 + 자 + 이.

How do you pronounce Vijay in Korean?

Say it bi-ja-i, with even weight on every syllable. ㅂ at the start is soft and unvoiced, halfway between p and b.

What does Vijay mean in Korean?

On its own, nothing: 비자이 spells the sound of Vijay, so the syllables are not Korean words and Koreans read it simply as a foreign name.

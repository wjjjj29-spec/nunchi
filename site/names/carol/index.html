Carol in Korean 캐럴

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Carol as 캐럴: two syllables, said kae-reol. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Carol letter by letter

Each Hangul block is one syllable, built from a consonant, a vowel and sometimes a final consonant underneath.

Pronunciation tip

ㄹ between two vowels (캐럴) is one quick tap of the tongue, like the tt in American "butter": closer to a soft d than an English r.

  • ㄹ at the bottom of 럴 is an l: the tongue touches just behind the teeth and stays there.
  • ㅓ (eo) in 럴 is the open "uh" of "sun", not an "o".
  • ㅐ (ae) is said like the e in "bed" by most Koreans today, a little more closed than the a in "cat".
  • ㅋ at the start is aspirated: a clear puff of air, like the k in "kite".

캐럴 nim. What you'll see on a delivery box or a booking.

Korean given names are almost always two syllables, so 캐럴 already sounds natural to Korean ears. In casual sentences Koreans often add 이 after a name that ends in a consonant: 캐럴이 왔어 ("Carol's here").

Spelled letter-by-letter you'd get 카롤, but Korean media write 캐럴, because the name is written the way it sounds, not the way it's spelled.

  • The unstressed "uh" vowel is written ㅓ (eo), whatever letter English uses for it.

Questions people ask

How do you write Carol in Korean?

Carol is written 캐럴 in Hangul (Revised Romanization: kaereol). It is the standard spelling Korean media use for the name. In Hangul it has two syllables: 캐 + 럴.

How do you pronounce Carol in Korean?

Say it kae-reol, with even weight on every syllable. ㄹ between two vowels (캐럴) is one quick tap of the tongue, like the tt in American "butter": closer to a soft d than an English r.

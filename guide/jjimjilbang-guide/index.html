How to visit a jjimjilbang: a first-timer's guide

Shoes off, clothes off, uniform on. How a Korean bathhouse works, what it costs in 2026 and how to spend the night for about ₩16,000.

4 min readChecked 20 October 2026
At a glance1 / 6
The short version
  • How it works: shoes in a locker, a soap shower, nude single-sex baths, then a uniform for the shared hot rooms and lounge.
  • Cost: about ₩10,000–16,000 to get into a Seoul jjimjilbang in 2026, a little more at night. A full body scrub is about ₩20,000–35,000 extra.
  • Overnight: most big ones are open 24 hours and you can sleep on the heated floor, around ₩16,000 a night. Under-19s need a guardian after 10pm.
  • Tattoos: each place sets its own rule. Small ones are rarely a problem; large pieces may get you turned away.

A 찜질방 (jjimjilbang, "heated room") is two places in one building. On their own floors are the single-sex baths, where everyone is naked. Elsewhere is a shared lounge where men, women and families relax in matching uniforms, sweat in hot rooms, eat, watch TV and sleep on the heated floor. Entry in Seoul is usually about ₩10,000–16,000 in 2026, and most big ones are open 24 hours.

Step by step

  1. Pay at the front desk. You get a locker key on a wristband, a uniform (찜질복, jjimjilbok) and a small towel or two. Snacks, drinks and scrubs usually go on the key, and you settle up when you leave.
  2. Shoes off first. Put them in the shoe locker by the entrance and keep the key.
  3. Go to your bath floor. Men's is 남탕 (namtang), women's is 여탕 (yeotang).
  4. Undress completely. Swimwear isn't allowed in the bath area, and nobody is looking.
  5. Shower with soap before you get in. Sit at a shower station and wash properly. Tie long hair up and keep towels out of the water.
  6. Soak. Tubs are hot (열탕, yeoltang), warm (온탕, ontang) and cold (냉탕, naengtang).
  7. Uniform on for the shared floor. Dry off, dress at your locker and head to the co-ed area: kiln-style hot rooms, cooler rooms, the snack bar and the sleeping floor.

Each venue sets its own prices. The two Sparex branches in Dongdaemun list about ₩13,000 by day and ₩16,000 at night. Scrub prices are fixed and posted in the bath area: pay that and don't tip. Small neighbourhood bathhouses can be cash-only, so read our cash and cards guide before you go.

Sleeping over

Sleeping on a mat on the heated 온돌 (ondol) floor is completely normal, and it's the cheapest bed in town. In February 2026 a Dongdaemun jjimjilbang manager told the Korea Economic Daily that a night there costs ₩16,000, against about ₩100,000 for a hotel. Bigger places have separate sleeping rooms, but expect snoring, lights and people coming and going. Bring earplugs and an eye mask.

  • Luggage: the Dongdaemun Sparex branches hold bags and let you go out once and come back within 24 hours of buying your ticket. Elsewhere, see luggage storage in Seoul.
  • Under-19s can't stay between 10pm and 5am without a guardian.
  • Not every place does nights. Some central spots, including one in Myeongdong, don't offer overnight stays, so ask at the desk.

Tattoos

No Korean law bans tattoos at bathhouses. Each place sets its own rule, usually worded as a ban on tattoos that make other guests uncomfortable (타인에게 불쾌감을 주는 문신). In practice small or discreet tattoos rarely draw comment, and newer, bigger places are more relaxed. Large pieces are more likely to get you asked to leave. If that happens, don't argue; try another place.

Eggs, sikhye and the lamb towel

The snack bar is half the point. Order 맥반석 계란 (maekbanseok gyeran), eggs baked on hot stones until the whites turn brown, and a cup of iced 식혜 (sikhye), a sweet rice drink. Then make the 양머리 (yangmeori, "lamb's head"): fold a small towel lengthwise, roll both ends in towards the middle and open it over your head so the rolls sit like ears. It keeps sweaty hair off your face in the hot rooms, and it's the photo everyone wants.

Nunchi tip

Your locker key is your wallet. Anything you buy inside goes on its number, so leave your phone and cards in the locker and pay once on the way out.

Where to go in Seoul

  • Sparex Dongmyo (스파렉스 동묘점): right by Exit 6 of Dongmyo Station (Lines 1 and 6), open 24 hours.
  • Sparex Good Morning City: near Exit 14 of Dongdaemun History & Culture Park Station, open 24 hours, with luggage storage. Handy if you're shopping at Dongdaemun at night.
  • Aquafield: newer, more polished spa and jjimjilbang venues run by Chosun Hotels & Resorts at Hanam and Goyang, just outside Seoul. About one in five guests there is now foreign.

Older guides still recommend some places that have since closed or changed hands. Check Naver Map or Kakao Map before you go.

Questions people ask

How much is a jjimjilbang in Seoul?

Usually about ₩10,000–16,000 for day entry in 2026, with night rates a few thousand won higher. A neighbourhood bathhouse with baths only is about ₩5,000–8,000. Scrubs, food and drinks cost extra.

Do you have to be naked in a jjimjilbang?

Only in the single-sex bath area, where swimwear isn't allowed and you shower before getting in. In the shared hot rooms and lounge everyone wears the uniform you're given at the desk.

Can tourists sleep overnight in a jjimjilbang?

Yes. Most large jjimjilbang are open 24 hours and sleeping on mats on the heated floor is normal. A night costs about ₩16,000 at Dongdaemun. Under-19s need a guardian between 10pm and 5am.

Can I go to a jjimjilbang with tattoos?

Often, yes. There's no law against it; each place sets its own rule. Small tattoos rarely draw comment, but large pieces may get you asked to leave, especially at older places.

What should I bring to a jjimjilbang?

Not much. The uniform and small towels come with your ticket. Bring a hair tie, your own skincare, and earplugs if you're staying the night. Some small bathhouses only take cash.

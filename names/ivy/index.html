Ivy in Korean 아이비

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Ivy as 아이비: three syllables, said a-i-bi. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Pronunciation tip

ㅇ at the top of 이 is silent, so 이 is just the vowel: i.

  • Give all 3 syllables the same weight. Korean has no strong stress, so it's a-i-bi, evenly.

How Koreans would call you

Friends calling you over
아이비야!
아이비 ends in a vowel, so it takes 야. Casual, for close friends.
At work or with new people
아이비 씨
아이비 ssi. Polite, roughly "Mr" or "Ms", but used with the first name.
Customer, client, online
아이비 님
아이비 nim. What you'll see on a delivery box or a booking.

Korean given names are almost always two syllables (after a one-syllable family name), so at 3 syllables 아이비 reads as a foreign name at a glance. With foreign names some Koreans skip 야 and just say 아이비 or 아이비 씨.

Why it's spelled 아이비

How Koreans write it
아이비
the settled media spelling

Spelled letter-by-letter you'd get 이비, but Korean media write 아이비, because the name is written the way it sounds, not the way it's spelled.

  • There is no v in Korean either: v becomes ㅂ (b).
  • The long i (as in "my") is spelled 아이: two beats in Korean, one in English.

Questions people ask

How do you write Ivy in Korean?

Ivy is written 아이비 in Hangul (Revised Romanization: aibi). It is the standard spelling Korean media use for the name. In Hangul it has three syllables: 아 + 이 + 비.

How do you pronounce Ivy in Korean?

Say it a-i-bi, with even weight on every syllable. ㅇ at the top of 이 is silent, so 이 is just the vowel: i.

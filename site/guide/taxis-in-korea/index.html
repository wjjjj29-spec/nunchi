Taking taxis in Korea: fares, apps and what to say

₩4,800 to start, 40% more after 11pm, and an app that takes your card. How to ride Seoul's taxis without overpaying.

The short version
  • Fare: Seoul taxis start at ₩4,800 for 1.6 km. Add 20% from 10pm and 40% from 11pm to 2am. No tipping.
  • Apps: k.ride (Kakao's visitor app) and Uber work with a foreign phone and card. Kakao T has the most cars but often rejects foreign cards.

What a Seoul taxi costs

A regular Seoul taxi starts at ₩4,800 for the first 1.6 km, then ₩100 for every 131 m, or every 30 seconds when you're crawling in traffic. The fare is rounded to the nearest ₩10. That rate dates from February 2023 and is still current in 2026; older guides quoting ₩3,800 and 2 km are out of date.

Leaving Seoul adds 20% for the part outside the city, and night and out-of-city surcharges together are capped at 60%. Other cities set their own fares. There's no tipping, and the driver can only charge what the meter shows.

To flag one down, look for a red 빈차 (bincha, empty car) light in the windscreen.

Deluxe and large taxis cost more per kilometre (₩200 for every 151 m) but have no 40% late-night tier.

International Taxi is Seoul's service for visitors, with drivers who speak English, Chinese or Japanese. Book at least 24 hours ahead at intltaxi.co.kr or on 1644-2255, or ask at the information booth in either Incheon terminal on the day. Besides airport runs, it does hourly hire from ₩70,000 for 3 hours, tolls extra. Drivers take cash or Visa, Mastercard and Amex.

Apps that work with a foreign phone and card

  • k.ride is Kakao Mobility's app for visitors. It runs in English, Chinese and Japanese, you sign in with email, Google or Apple, and you pay with a foreign card you register first. It translates your chat with the driver. It doesn't offer Kakao T's cheapest option, and adds a service fee.
  • Uber works with your home account and sends licensed taxis, not private cars. It's the former UT, rebranded in 2024. Most cars are in Seoul.
  • Kakao T is what Koreans use and has the most cars. It's built around Korean accounts and foreign cards are often declined. If you use it, pick the option to pay the driver directly.

Nunchi tip

Booked in an app and want to go somewhere else? Change the destination in the app, not just with the driver. In May 2026 a tourist who switched destination mid-ride on an Uber was asked to pay again on the card terminal and charged ₩690,800 for a ride of about ₩70,000.

Paying

Taxis have a card terminal by the front seats. Most take credit cards and T-money: tap or insert when you arrive. Some foreign cards get rejected, so carry a topped-up T-money card or a few ₩10,000 notes as backup. If you're choosing a transit card, our T-money vs Climate Card tool helps. Seoul has been adding English to taxi receipts since early 2026, showing the fare, times and any surcharge.

Show the address in Korean

Drivers set the satnav from Korean. An English hotel name or romanised street often won't work. Copy your destination's Korean name or address from Naver Map and show the screen, or save your hotel's Korean address before you go out.

From the airport

At Incheon, use only the official taxi ranks outside arrivals, never someone who approaches you inside. A regular metered taxi to central Seoul comes to about ₩70,000–90,000 with tolls, more at night. International Taxi charges fixed fares from Incheon, tolls included:

Scams and how to report them

Most rides are fine. The problems Seoul city reports are drivers not switching on the meter, adding an out-of-city surcharge inside Seoul, charging more than the meter shows, demanding tips and refusing short trips. Make sure the meter is running when you set off, and check the amount on the terminal before you tap.

If something goes wrong, ask for the receipt, which shows the taxi's details, and photograph the driver's licence card on the dashboard. Then report it:

  • QR code: scan the code posted inside Seoul taxis to file a report in your language. Confirmed overcharging leads to fines.
  • 120 Dasan Call Centre: Seoul's city helpline, which handles taxi complaints. Dial 120.
  • 1330: the Korea Travel Hotline, for interpretation if you need help talking to the driver.

Questions people ask

How much is a taxi in Seoul?

A regular taxi starts at ₩4,800 for the first 1.6 km, then ₩100 for every 131 m or 30 seconds in traffic. It costs 20% more from 10pm to 4am and 40% more from 11pm to 2am.

Can tourists use Kakao T in Korea?

You can, but it's built around Korean accounts and many foreign cards are declined. Kakao Mobility's k.ride app is made for visitors, and Uber also books licensed taxis in Korea.

Can I pay for a taxi in Korea by card?

Usually. Taxis have a card terminal and most take credit cards and T-money. Some foreign cards fail, so keep T-money or a little cash as backup.

How much is a taxi from Incheon Airport to Seoul?

About ₩70,000–90,000 by meter including tolls, more at night. International Taxi has fixed fares: ₩85,000 to Myeongdong or Jongno and ₩90,000 to Gangnam.

How do I report a taxi driver in Seoul?

Keep the receipt, then scan the QR code inside the taxi to report in your language, or call the 120 Dasan Call Centre. Confirmed overcharging leads to fines.

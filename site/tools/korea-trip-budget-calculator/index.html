What the numbers are based on

We keep three price profiles and check them against current listings and menus. All figures are per person per day unless noted, in Korean won.

Backpacker means hostel dorms or guesthouses, kimbap and convenience-store dinners, the subway everywhere. Comfortable means a clean three-star or a good Airbnb near a station, sit-down Korean meals, a taxi when it rains. Treat yourself means a four-star in Myeongdong or Gangnam, tasting menus and taxis by default.

Where people overspend

Hotels in central Seoul. Prices jump in April, October and around holidays. Book two months out or stay one subway stop away from the famous area. Western food and cocktails. A pasta in Itaewon costs what a full Korean set meal costs three times over. Airport SIM counters. Buy an eSIM before you fly. Taxis from Incheon. The AREX express train is ₩11,000. A taxi is ₩70,000 or more.

Where Korea is cheap

Public transport is excellent and a ride is about ₩1,500. Convenience stores sell real meals. Street food in markets runs ₩3,000 to ₩6,000 a dish. Museums and palaces cost a few thousand won, and Gyeongbokgung is free if you wear a rented hanbok.

Budgeting from your country

Flights are the one cost we leave out of the calculator, so here is the missing piece for the places most of our readers fly from. Pick your currency above and the numbers in the results change to match. Flight bands are return economy fares, rough and seasonal.

From the Philippines 필리핀

Manila, Cebu and Clark all have direct flights to Incheon; Cebu Pacific, Jeju Air and Jin Air fares run roughly ₱10,000 to ₱25,000 return, more in Christmas and Holy Week peaks. Most Filipino travellers we hear from land between Backpacker and Comfortable and share a room between three or four friends, which drops the per-person number a lot. One practical note: Philippine passport holders still need a tourist visa, so apply through the Korea Visa Application Center six to eight weeks ahead. GCash now works at some Korean shops through Alipay+, but a Visa or Mastercard debit card is the thing you will actually use every day.

From India 인도

Direct flights from Delhi and Mumbai to Seoul are roughly ₹35,000 to ₹60,000 return; a connection in Bangkok, Hong Kong or Singapore is often cheaper and lets you add a stopover. Indian travellers mostly pick Comfortable, partly because hotel rooms are shared and partly because Korean transport is so cheap that the savings go into food and K-pop or drama experiences. UPI does not work in Korea, so bring a zero-forex-markup card or load a Wise or Niyo card before you fly. Vegetarians: look for temple food (사찰음식), bibimbap without meat, and tell staff "고기 빼고" (gogi ppaego, without meat).

From Malaysia 말레이시아

Kuala Lumpur to Seoul is roughly RM800 to RM1,800 return on AirAsia X, Batik Air or Malaysia Airlines, with the low end in the shoulder months of May, June and November. Comfortable is the usual pick, and Korea's subway and street food feel cheap next to KL taxis and mall dining. Touch 'n Go eWallet works at Alipay+ merchants in Seoul, but a physical card is still safer. Halal food is easiest around Itaewon and the Seoul Central Mosque; download the Halal Korea app before you arrive.

From Singapore 싱가포르

Singapore to Seoul is roughly S$450 to S$900 return, Scoot at the low end and Singapore Airlines or Korean Air at the top. Singaporeans usually go Comfortable or Treat yourself, and still find Seoul cheaper than home for food, coffee and getting around. Bring a YouTrip, Trust or Revolut card and skip the airport money changers; cards work almost everywhere. Buy a Korea eSIM before you fly and your phone works the moment you land.

From Indonesia 인도네시아

Jakarta to Seoul is roughly Rp5 to Rp10 million return; Garuda and Korean Air fly direct, while AirAsia or Scoot via Kuala Lumpur or Singapore can be cheaper if you have a day to spare. Indonesian travellers mostly land between Backpacker and Comfortable and travel in groups, which spreads the room cost. Indonesian passport holders need a tourist visa, so start early. Do not bring rupiah to exchange in Korea; the rates are poor and many changers refuse it. Carry US dollars or, better, a Jenius, Jago or Wise card.

Questions people ask

Does this include flights?

No. Flights vary too much by origin and season. Add your fare on top of the total.

Is cash still needed in Korea?

Rarely. Cards work almost everywhere, including street stalls with a terminal. Carry ₩50,000 to ₩100,000 in cash for markets and small shops, and load a T-money card for transit.

What exchange rate do you use?

A planning rate of roughly ₩1,380 per US dollar, and similar rounded rates for the other currencies in the dropdown (₱, ₹, RM, S$, Rp, ₫, ฿, £, €, A$, C$, ¥). They are there to give you a feel for the number, not to settle a bill. Check the live rate before you travel. Your card will use the rate on the day.

Can I see the budget in my own currency?

Yes. Pick your currency in the "Show it in my currency" dropdown and the totals and each line of the table show it next to the won. We remember your choice on this device, and you can share a link like ?cur=PHP that opens the page already set.

Are prices different in Busan or Jeju?

Hotels are usually 10 to 20 percent cheaper than Seoul outside peak season. Jeju needs a rental car, which adds ₩50,000 to ₩80,000 a day.

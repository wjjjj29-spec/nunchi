Russell in Korean 러셀

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Russell as 러셀: two syllables, said reo-sel. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Russell letter by letter

Each Hangul block is one syllable, built from a consonant, a vowel and sometimes a final consonant underneath.

Pronunciation tip

The opening ㄹ is a single light tap, halfway between an English r and l. Never roll it.

  • ㄹ at the bottom of 셀 is an l: the tongue touches just behind the teeth and stays there.
  • ㅓ (eo) in 러 is the open "uh" of "sun", not an "o".
  • Two even beats, reo-sel: Korean doesn't stress one syllable over the other the way English does.

러셀 ssi. Polite, roughly "Mr" or "Ms", but used with the first name.
Customer, client, online
러셀 님
러셀 nim. What you'll see on a delivery box or a booking.

Korean given names are almost always two syllables, so 러셀 already sounds natural to Korean ears. In casual sentences Koreans often add 이 after a name that ends in a consonant: 러셀이 왔어 ("Russell's here").

Why it's spelled 러셀

Here the letters and the sound line up: applying the loanword rules letter by letter already gives 러셀, the same spelling Korean media use.

  • The unstressed "uh" vowel is written ㅓ (eo), whatever letter English uses for it.

Questions people ask

How do you write Russell in Korean?

Russell is written 러셀 in Hangul (Revised Romanization: reosel). It is the standard spelling Korean media use for the name. In Hangul it has two syllables: 러 + 셀.

How do you pronounce Russell in Korean?

Say it reo-sel, with even weight on every syllable. The opening ㄹ is a single light tap, halfway between an English r and l. Never roll it.

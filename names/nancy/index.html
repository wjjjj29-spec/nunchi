Nancy in Korean 낸시

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Nancy as 낸시: two syllables, said naen-si. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Nancy letter by letter

Each Hangul block is one syllable, built from a consonant, a vowel and sometimes a final consonant underneath.

Pronunciation tip

ㅐ (ae) is said like the e in "bed" by most Koreans today, a little more closed than the a in "cat".

  • Two even beats, naen-si: Korean doesn't stress one syllable over the other the way English does.

How Koreans would call you

Friends calling you over
낸시야!
낸시 ends in a vowel, so it takes 야. Casual, for close friends.
At work or with new people
낸시 씨
낸시 ssi. Polite, roughly "Mr" or "Ms", but used with the first name.
Customer, client, online
낸시 님
낸시 nim. What you'll see on a delivery box or a booking.

Korean given names are almost always two syllables, so 낸시 already sounds natural to Korean ears. With foreign names some Koreans skip 야 and just say 낸시 or 낸시 씨.

Why it's spelled 낸시

Here the letters and the sound line up: applying the loanword rules letter by letter already gives 낸시, the same spelling Korean media use.

Questions people ask

How do you write Nancy in Korean?

Nancy is written 낸시 in Hangul (Revised Romanization: naensi). It is the standard spelling Korean media use for the name. In Hangul it has two syllables: 낸 + 시.

How do you pronounce Nancy in Korean?

Say it naen-si, with even weight on every syllable. ㅐ (ae) is said like the e in "bed" by most Koreans today, a little more closed than the a in "cat".

Eric in Korean 에릭

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Eric as 에릭: two syllables, said e-rik. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Pronunciation tip

ㄹ between two vowels (에릭) is one quick tap of the tongue, like the tt in American "butter": closer to a soft d than an English r.

  • The final ㄱ in 릭 is unreleased: stop the sound (k) without a puff of air.
  • Two even beats, e-rik: Korean doesn't stress one syllable over the other the way English does.

Korean given names are almost always two syllables, so 에릭 already sounds natural to Korean ears. In casual sentences Koreans often add 이 after a name that ends in a consonant: 에릭이 왔어 ("Eric's here").

Why it's spelled 에릭

Here the letters and the sound line up: applying the loanword rules letter by letter already gives 에릭, the same spelling Korean media use.

  • After a short vowel the final k tucks under the syllable as ㄱ, instead of getting its own syllable.

Questions people ask

How do you write Eric in Korean?

Eric is written 에릭 in Hangul (Revised Romanization: erik). It is the standard spelling Korean media use for the name. In Hangul it has two syllables: 에 + 릭.

How do you pronounce Eric in Korean?

Say it e-rik, with even weight on every syllable. ㄹ between two vowels (에릭) is one quick tap of the tongue, like the tt in American "butter": closer to a soft d than an English r.

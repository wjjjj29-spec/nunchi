What the numbers mean

타/분Roughly
100–200Learner. You are finding the keys.
200–350Comfortable. Chatting on KakaoTalk without pain.
350–500Korean office worker. Typical adult speed.
500–700Fast. PC-bang veteran or someone who grew up on MSN.
700+Show-off. Koreans will ask you to type in front of them.

타 (ta) counts keystrokes, not letters. A syllable like 한 is three strokes (ㅎ, ㅏ, ㄴ), which is why Korean speeds look higher than English words-per-minute. We count strokes the way Korean typing programs do: one per jamo, with double consonants and compound vowels counting as two.

Getting faster

Consonants sit on the left of the keyboard, vowels on the right; your left hand does most of the work. Learn ㅐ (o) and ㅔ (p) positions early, they are the most common mistakes. Do not fight autocorrect on your phone: this test is for a real keyboard. Ten minutes a day on this page for two weeks moves most learners from the first row of the table to the second.

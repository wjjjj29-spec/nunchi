Raymond in Korean 레이먼드

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Raymond as 레이먼드: four syllables, said re-i-meon-deu. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Pronunciation tip

The opening ㄹ is a single light tap, halfway between an English r and l. Never roll it.

  • 드 carries the filler vowel ㅡ (eu). Keep it short and light, almost just the consonant.
  • ㅓ (eo) in 먼 is the open "uh" of "sun", not an "o".
  • ㅇ at the top of 이 is silent, so 이 is just the vowel: i.
  • Give all 4 syllables the same weight. Korean has no strong stress, so it's re-i-meon-deu, evenly.

Korean given names are almost always two syllables (after a one-syllable family name), so at 4 syllables 레이먼드 reads as a foreign name at a glance. With foreign names some Koreans skip 야 and just say 레이먼드 or 레이먼드 씨.

Why it's spelled 레이먼드

How Koreans write it
레이먼드

Spelled letter-by-letter you'd get 레이몬드, but Korean media write 레이먼드, because the name is written the way it sounds, not the way it's spelled.

  • The long a (as in "day") is spelled as two beats, 레이, because Korean writes each part of the diphthong.
  • The unstressed "uh" vowel is written ㅓ (eo), whatever letter English uses for it.
  • Korean adds the vowel ㅡ to consonants that have no vowel of their own (드), which is why Raymond has 4 syllables in Korean.

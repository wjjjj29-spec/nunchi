Rob in Korean 롭

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Rob as 롭: one syllable, said rop. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Rob in Hangul
롭

Rob letter by letter

Each Hangul block is one syllable, built from a consonant, a vowel and sometimes a final consonant underneath.

롭
ㄹ r/l + ㅗ o + ㅂ p (final)
rop
Pronunciation tip

The opening ㄹ is a single light tap, halfway between an English r and l. Never roll it.

  • The final ㅂ in 롭 is unreleased: stop the sound (p) without a puff of air.
  • ㅗ is a pure "o" with rounded lips. Don't add the w-glide English puts in "go".
  • It's a single syllable in Korean, so say it in one short beat: rop.

How Koreans would call you

롭 ends in a consonant, so it takes 아, said 로바. Casual, for close friends.
At work or with new people
롭 씨
롭 ssi. Polite, roughly "Mr" or "Ms", but used with the first name.
Customer, client, online
롭 님
롭 nim. What you'll see on a delivery box or a booking.

One syllable is short for Korea, where given names are almost always two, so 롭 stands out and is easy to remember. In casual sentences Koreans often add 이 after a name that ends in a consonant: 롭이 왔어 ("Rob's here").

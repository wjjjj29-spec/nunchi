Felix in Korean 펠릭스

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Felix as 펠릭스: three syllables, said pel-lik-seu. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Pronunciation tip

The double ㄹ in 펠릭 is a clear English l, as in "hello", not an r.

  • 스 carries the filler vowel ㅡ (eu). Keep it short and light, almost just the consonant.
  • The final ㄱ in 릭 is unreleased: stop the sound (k) without a puff of air.
  • ㅍ at the start is aspirated: a clear puff of air, like the p in "pie".
  • Give all 3 syllables the same weight. Korean has no strong stress, so it's pel-lik-seu, evenly.

Korean given names are almost always two syllables (after a one-syllable family name), so at 3 syllables 펠릭스 reads as a foreign name at a glance. With foreign names some Koreans skip 야 and just say 펠릭스 or 펠릭스 씨.

Why it's spelled 펠릭스

Here the letters and the sound line up: applying the loanword rules letter by letter already gives 펠릭스, the same spelling Korean media use.

  • Korean has no f sound, so f becomes ㅍ (p), said with a puff of air.
  • x is two sounds, k and s, so Hangul writes both: ㄱ (k) and ㅅ (s).
  • An l between vowels is written twice (final ㄹ + ㄹ), so Koreans say a clear l instead of a tapped r.
  • Korean adds the vowel ㅡ to consonants that have no vowel of their own (스), which is why Felix has 3 syllables in Korean.

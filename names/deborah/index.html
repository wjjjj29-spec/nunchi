Deborah in Korean 데버라

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Deborah as 데버라: three syllables, said de-beo-ra. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Pronunciation tip

ㄹ between two vowels (버라) is one quick tap of the tongue, like the tt in American "butter": closer to a soft d than an English r.

  • ㅓ (eo) in 버 is the open "uh" of "sun", not an "o".
  • ㄷ at the start is soft and unvoiced, halfway between t and d.
  • Give all 3 syllables the same weight. Korean has no strong stress, so it's de-beo-ra, evenly.

How Koreans would call you

Friends calling you over
데버라야!
데버라 ends in a vowel, so it takes 야. Casual, for close friends.
At work or with new people
데버라 씨
데버라 ssi. Polite, roughly "Mr" or "Ms", but used with the first name.
Customer, client, online
데버라 님
데버라 nim. What you'll see on a delivery box or a booking.

Korean given names are almost always two syllables (after a one-syllable family name), so at 3 syllables 데버라 reads as a foreign name at a glance. With foreign names some Koreans skip 야 and just say 데버라 or 데버라 씨.

Why it's spelled 데버라

How Koreans write it
데버라
the settled media spelling

Spelled letter-by-letter you'd get 데보라, but Korean media write 데버라, because the name is written the way it sounds, not the way it's spelled.

  • The final h is silent and is dropped.
  • The unstressed "uh" vowel is written ㅓ (eo), whatever letter English uses for it.

Santiago in Korean 산티아고

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Santiago as 산티아고: four syllables, said san-ti-a-go. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Pronunciation tip

ㅗ is a pure "o" with rounded lips. Don't add the w-glide English puts in "go".

  • ㅇ at the top of 아 is silent, so 아 is just the vowel: a.
  • Give all 4 syllables the same weight. Korean has no strong stress, so it's san-ti-a-go, evenly.

How Koreans would call you

Friends calling you over
산티아고야!
산티아고 ends in a vowel, so it takes 야. Casual, for close friends.
At work or with new people
산티아고 씨
산티아고 ssi. Polite, roughly "Mr" or "Ms", but used with the first name.
Customer, client, online
산티아고 님
산티아고 nim. What you'll see on a delivery box or a booking.

Korean given names are almost always two syllables (after a one-syllable family name), so at 4 syllables 산티아고 reads as a foreign name at a glance. With foreign names some Koreans skip 야 and just say 산티아고 or 산티아고 씨.

Why it's spelled 산티아고

Here the letters and the sound line up: applying the loanword rules letter by letter already gives 산티아고, the same spelling Korean media use.

  • Korean writes Spanish names by their Spanish sound: vowels are read as written.

Questions people ask

How do you write Santiago in Korean?

Santiago is written 산티아고 in Hangul (Revised Romanization: santiago). It is the standard spelling Korean media use for the name and follows the Spanish pronunciation. In Hangul it has four syllables: 산 + 티 + 아 + 고.

How do you pronounce Santiago in Korean?

Say it san-ti-a-go, with even weight on every syllable. ㅗ is a pure "o" with rounded lips. Don't add the w-glide English puts in "go".

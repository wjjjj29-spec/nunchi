Levi in Korean 리바이

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Levi as 리바이: three syllables, said ri-ba-i. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Pronunciation tip

The opening ㄹ is a single light tap, halfway between an English r and l. Never roll it.

  • ㅇ at the top of 이 is silent, so 이 is just the vowel: i.
  • Give all 3 syllables the same weight. Korean has no strong stress, so it's ri-ba-i, evenly.

How Koreans would call you

Friends calling you over
리바이야!
리바이 ends in a vowel, so it takes 야. Casual, for close friends.
At work or with new people
리바이 씨
리바이 ssi. Polite, roughly "Mr" or "Ms", but used with the first name.
Customer, client, online
리바이 님
리바이 nim. What you'll see on a delivery box or a booking.

Korean given names are almost always two syllables (after a one-syllable family name), so at 3 syllables 리바이 reads as a foreign name at a glance. With foreign names some Koreans skip 야 and just say 리바이 or 리바이 씨.

Why it's spelled 리바이

How Koreans write it
리바이
the settled media spelling

Spelled letter-by-letter you'd get 레비, but Korean media write 리바이, because the name is written the way it sounds, not the way it's spelled.

  • There is no v in Korean either: v becomes ㅂ (b).
  • The long i (as in "my") is spelled 아이: two beats in Korean, one in English.

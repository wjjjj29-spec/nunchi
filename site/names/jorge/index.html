Jorge in Korean 호르헤

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Jorge as 호르헤: three syllables, said ho-reu-he. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Pronunciation tip

ㄹ between two vowels (호르) is one quick tap of the tongue, like the tt in American "butter": closer to a soft d than an English r.

  • 르 carries the filler vowel ㅡ (eu). Keep it short and light, almost just the consonant.
  • ㅗ is a pure "o" with rounded lips. Don't add the w-glide English puts in "go".
  • Give all 3 syllables the same weight. Korean has no strong stress, so it's ho-reu-he, evenly.

호르헤 님
호르헤 nim. What you'll see on a delivery box or a booking.

Korean given names are almost always two syllables (after a one-syllable family name), so at 3 syllables 호르헤 reads as a foreign name at a glance. With foreign names some Koreans skip 야 and just say 호르헤 or 호르헤 씨.

Spelled letter-by-letter you'd get 조제, but Korean media write 호르헤, because the name is written the way it sounds in Spanish, not the way it's spelled.

  • Korean writes Spanish names by their Spanish sound: vowels are read as written, j is an h sound (ㅎ) and the r is pronounced (ㄹ/르) instead of being dropped as in English.

Questions people ask

How do you write Jorge in Korean?

Jorge is written 호르헤 in Hangul (Revised Romanization: horeuhe). It is the standard spelling Korean media use for the name and follows the Spanish pronunciation. In Hangul it has three syllables: 호 + 르 + 헤.

How do you pronounce Jorge in Korean?

Say it ho-reu-he, with even weight on every syllable. ㄹ between two vowels (호르) is one quick tap of the tongue, like the tt in American "butter": closer to a soft d than an English r.

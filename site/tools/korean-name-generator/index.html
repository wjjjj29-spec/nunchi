How Korean names work

Surname first, given name second: Kim Seo-yeon, Park Do-yun. The surname is inherited and there are not many of them; Kim, Lee and Park alone cover almost half the country. The given name is where the meaning lives. Most are two syllables, and each syllable is picked for its hanja (Chinese character): 서 can mean "auspicious" (瑞), 연 "lotus" (蓮) or "beautiful" (姸), so 서연 reads as something like "a beautiful good omen".

That is why this generator asks you to describe yourself. One sound has many characters: 하 can be summer (夏) or river (河), 현 can be wise (賢) or shining (炫). Your words decide which one you get, and the card tells you which word picked it.

Since the 1990s, pure-Korean names with no hanja have become popular too: 하늘 (sky), 이슬 (dew), 다온 (all good things come), 라온 (joyful). They sound soft and modern and are easy for foreigners to say. When one fits your description, we show it as well.

Using a Korean name as a foreigner

Perfectly normal. Teachers, exchange students and long-term residents often pick one, and colleagues use it happily. Two habits make it work. First, keep your passport name on anything official; the Korean name is social. Second, pick a surname that starts with the same sound as your real name (Kevin → 김, Sophie → 서 or 송) so it feels like yours and not a costume. Koreans will ask what it means. Have the answer ready; it is the best small talk you will ever do.

Questions people ask

How are Korean names structured?

Surname, then given name. Usually one plus two syllables. Written together in Hangul (김서연), romanised with a hyphen or space in the given name (Kim Seo-yeon).

How do you choose the hanja?

We read the words you type and match them to meanings (ocean, courage, books, morning and a few hundred more). Then we look for hanja with that dictionary meaning that Korean parents actually use in given names, and pair two into a name real Koreans have, so it sounds natural rather than invented. Each card shows which of your words picked which character.

Can I use this name officially?

Treat it as a social name: class, work, friends, your Instagram bio. As a foreigner your legal name in Korea is your passport name, and that is what goes on your residence card, bank account and contracts. If you ever naturalise you can register a Korean name; its hanja must come from the official list of characters allowed in names (인명용 한자), so have it checked before you file.

Can I just transliterate my real name instead?

Yes, and for documents you should. Try My Name in Hangul. A Korean name is for the social side.

Do Korean names have a gender?

Softly. Some names lean feminine (서연, 하은), some masculine (도윤, 민준), and many are used for both (지우, 하람, 유진). We label the lean but nothing is a rule.

Is it strange for a foreigner to use a Korean name?

No. It is usually taken as a sign you care. The only misstep is choosing a celebrity's full name; that reads like a joke.

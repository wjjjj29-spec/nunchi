Nick in Korean 닉

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Nick as 닉: one syllable, said nik. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Nick in Hangul
닉
Revised Romanization: nik · said nik · one syllable

Nick letter by letter

Each Hangul block is one syllable, built from a consonant, a vowel and sometimes a final consonant underneath.

닉
ㄴ n + ㅣ i + ㄱ k (final)
nik
Pronunciation tip

The final ㄱ in 닉 is unreleased: stop the sound (k) without a puff of air.

  • It's a single syllable in Korean, so say it in one short beat: nik.

How Koreans would call you

Friends calling you over
닉아!
닉 ends in a consonant, so it takes 아, said 니가. Casual, for close friends.
At work or with new people
닉 씨
닉 ssi. Polite, roughly "Mr" or "Ms", but used with the first name.
Customer, client, online
닉 님
닉 nim. What you'll see on a delivery box or a booking.

One syllable is short for Korea, where given names are almost always two, so 닉 stands out and is easy to remember. In casual sentences Koreans often add 이 after a name that ends in a consonant: 닉이 왔어 ("Nick's here").

Good to know

Calling Nick over, friends say 닉아, which comes out as 니가, the casual word for "you". It's a small, reliable joke.

Why it's spelled 닉

Here the letters and the sound line up: applying the loanword rules letter by letter already gives 닉, the same spelling Korean media use.

  • After a short vowel the final k tucks under the syllable as ㄱ, instead of getting its own syllable.

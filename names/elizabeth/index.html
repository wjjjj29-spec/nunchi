Elizabeth in Korean 엘리자베스

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Elizabeth as 엘리자베스: five syllables, said el-li-ja-be-seu. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Pronunciation tip

The double ㄹ in 엘리 is a clear English l, as in "hello", not an r.

  • 스 carries the filler vowel ㅡ (eu). Keep it short and light, almost just the consonant.
  • Give all 5 syllables the same weight. Korean has no strong stress, so it's el-li-ja-be-seu, evenly.

Korean given names are almost always two syllables (after a one-syllable family name), so at 5 syllables 엘리자베스 reads as a foreign name at a glance. With foreign names some Koreans skip 야 and just say 엘리자베스 or 엘리자베스 씨.

Why it's spelled 엘리자베스

Here the letters and the sound line up: applying the loanword rules letter by letter already gives 엘리자베스, the same spelling Korean media use.

  • Korean has no th, so th becomes ㅅ (s), the closest sound.
  • Korean has no z, so z becomes ㅈ (j).
  • An l between vowels is written twice (final ㄹ + ㄹ), so Koreans say a clear l instead of a tapped r.
  • Korean adds the vowel ㅡ to consonants that have no vowel of their own (스), which is why Elizabeth has 5 syllables in Korean.

How do you pronounce Elizabeth in Korean?

Say it el-li-ja-be-seu, with even weight on every syllable. The double ㄹ in 엘리 is a clear English l, as in "hello", not an r.

Sam in Korean 샘

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Sam as 샘: one syllable, said saem. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Sam in Hangul
샘
Revised Romanization: saem · said saem · one syllable

Sam letter by letter

Each Hangul block is one syllable, built from a consonant, a vowel and sometimes a final consonant underneath.

샘
ㅅ s + ㅐ ae + ㅁ m (final)
saem
Pronunciation tip

ㅐ (ae) is said like the e in "bed" by most Koreans today, a little more closed than the a in "cat".

  • It's a single syllable in Korean, so say it in one short beat: saem.

How Koreans would call you

Friends calling you over
샘아!
샘 ends in a consonant, so it takes 아, said 새마. Casual, for close friends.
At work or with new people
샘 씨
샘 ssi. Polite, roughly "Mr" or "Ms", but used with the first name.
Customer, client, online
샘 님
샘 nim. What you'll see on a delivery box or a booking.

One syllable is short for Korea, where given names are almost always two, so 샘 stands out and is easy to remember. In casual sentences Koreans often add 이 after a name that ends in a consonant: 샘이 왔어 ("Sam's here").

Good to know

샘 means a spring (of water) in Korean, and 샘내다 means to be jealous.

Why it's spelled 샘

Here the letters and the sound line up: applying the loanword rules letter by letter already gives 샘, the same spelling Korean media use.

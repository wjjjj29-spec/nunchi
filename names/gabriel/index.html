Pronunciation tip

ㄹ between two vowels (브리) is one quick tap of the tongue, like the tt in American "butter": closer to a soft d than an English r.

  • 브 carries the filler vowel ㅡ (eu). Keep it short and light, almost just the consonant.
  • ㄹ at the bottom of 엘 is an l: the tongue touches just behind the teeth and stays there.
  • ㄱ at the start is soft and unvoiced, halfway between k and g.
  • ㅇ at the top of 엘 is silent, so 엘 is just the vowel: el.

Korean given names are almost always two syllables (after a one-syllable family name), so at 4 syllables 가브리엘 reads as a foreign name at a glance. In casual sentences Koreans often add 이 after a name that ends in a consonant: 가브리엘이 왔어 ("Gabriel's here").

Spelled letter-by-letter you'd get 개브릴, but Korean media write 가브리엘, because the name is written the way it sounds, not the way it's spelled.

  • Korean adds the vowel ㅡ to consonants that have no vowel of their own (브), which is why Gabriel has 4 syllables in Korean.

How do you pronounce Gabriel in Korean?

Say it ga-beu-ri-el, with even weight on every syllable. ㄹ between two vowels (브리) is one quick tap of the tongue, like the tt in American "butter": closer to a soft d than an English r.

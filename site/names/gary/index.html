Gary in Korean 게리

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Gary as 게리: two syllables, said ge-ri. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Gary letter by letter

Each Hangul block is one syllable, built from a consonant, a vowel and sometimes a final consonant underneath.

Pronunciation tip

ㄹ between two vowels (게리) is one quick tap of the tongue, like the tt in American "butter": closer to a soft d than an English r.

  • ㄱ at the start is soft and unvoiced, halfway between k and g.
  • Two even beats, ge-ri: Korean doesn't stress one syllable over the other the way English does.

How Koreans would call you

Friends calling you over
게리야!
게리 ends in a vowel, so it takes 야. Casual, for close friends.
At work or with new people
게리 씨
게리 ssi. Polite, roughly "Mr" or "Ms", but used with the first name.
Customer, client, online
게리 님
게리 nim. What you'll see on a delivery box or a booking.

Korean given names are almost always two syllables, so 게리 already sounds natural to Korean ears. With foreign names some Koreans skip 야 and just say 게리 or 게리 씨.

Questions people ask

How do you write Gary in Korean?

Gary is written 게리 in Hangul (Revised Romanization: geri). It is the standard spelling Korean media use for the name. In Hangul it has two syllables: 게 + 리.

How do you pronounce Gary in Korean?

Say it ge-ri, with even weight on every syllable. ㄹ between two vowels (게리) is one quick tap of the tongue, like the tt in American "butter": closer to a soft d than an English r.

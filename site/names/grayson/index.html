Pronunciation tip

ㄹ between two vowels (그레) is one quick tap of the tongue, like the tt in American "butter": closer to a soft d than an English r.

  • 그 and 슨 carry the filler vowel ㅡ (eu). Keep them short and light, almost just the consonant.
  • ㄱ at the start is soft and unvoiced, halfway between k and g.
  • ㅇ at the top of 이 is silent, so 이 is just the vowel: i.
  • Give all 4 syllables the same weight. Korean has no strong stress, so it's geu-re-i-seun, evenly.

Korean given names are almost always two syllables (after a one-syllable family name), so at 4 syllables 그레이슨 reads as a foreign name at a glance. In casual sentences Koreans often add 이 after a name that ends in a consonant: 그레이슨이 왔어 ("Grayson's here").

How Koreans write it

Spelled letter-by-letter you'd get 그레이손, but Korean media write 그레이슨, because the name is written the way it sounds, not the way it's spelled.

  • The long a (as in "day") is spelled as two beats, 레이, because Korean writes each part of the diphthong.
  • Korean adds the vowel ㅡ to consonants that have no vowel of their own (그 and 슨), which is why Grayson has 4 syllables in Korean.

Shiva in Korean 시바

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Shiva as 시바: two syllables, said si-ba. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Shiva in Hangul
시바
Revised Romanization: siba · said si-ba · two syllables

Shiva letter by letter

Each Hangul block is one syllable, built from a consonant, a vowel and sometimes a final consonant underneath.

바
ㅂ b + ㅏ a

Pronunciation tip

Two even beats, si-ba: Korean doesn't stress one syllable over the other the way English does.

How Koreans would call you

Friends calling you over
시바야!
시바 ends in a vowel, so it takes 야. Casual, for close friends.
At work or with new people
시바 씨
시바 ssi. Polite, roughly "Mr" or "Ms", but used with the first name.
Customer, client, online
시바 님
시바 nim. What you'll see on a delivery box or a booking.

Korean given names are almost always two syllables, so 시바 already sounds natural to Korean ears. With foreign names some Koreans skip 야 and just say 시바 or 시바 씨.

Why it's spelled 시바

Here the letters and the sound line up: applying the loanword rules letter by letter already gives 시바, the same spelling Korean media use.

  • There is no v in Korean either: v becomes ㅂ (b).

Questions people ask

How do you write Shiva in Korean?

Shiva is written 시바 in Hangul (Revised Romanization: siba). It is the standard spelling Korean media use for the name. In Hangul it has two syllables: 시 + 바.

How do you pronounce Shiva in Korean?

Say it si-ba, with even weight on every syllable. Two even beats, si-ba: Korean doesn't stress one syllable over the other the way English does.

What does Shiva mean in Korean?

On its own, nothing: 시바 spells the sound of Shiva, so the syllables are not Korean words and Koreans read it simply as a foreign name.

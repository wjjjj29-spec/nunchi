Judith in Korean 주디스

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Judith as 주디스: three syllables, said ju-di-seu. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Pronunciation tip

스 carries the filler vowel ㅡ (eu). Keep it short and light, almost just the consonant.

  • ㅈ at the start is soft and unvoiced, halfway between ch and j.
  • Give all 3 syllables the same weight. Korean has no strong stress, so it's ju-di-seu, evenly.

How Koreans would call you

Friends calling you over
주디스야!
주디스 ends in a vowel, so it takes 야. Casual, for close friends.
At work or with new people
주디스 씨
주디스 ssi. Polite, roughly "Mr" or "Ms", but used with the first name.
Customer, client, online
주디스 님
주디스 nim. What you'll see on a delivery box or a booking.

Korean given names are almost always two syllables (after a one-syllable family name), so at 3 syllables 주디스 reads as a foreign name at a glance. With foreign names some Koreans skip 야 and just say 주디스 or 주디스 씨.

Why it's spelled 주디스

Here the letters and the sound line up: applying the loanword rules letter by letter already gives 주디스, the same spelling Korean media use.

  • Korean has no th, so th becomes ㅅ (s), the closest sound.
  • Korean adds the vowel ㅡ to consonants that have no vowel of their own (스), which is why Judith has 3 syllables in Korean.

Questions people ask

How do you write Judith in Korean?

Judith is written 주디스 in Hangul (Revised Romanization: judiseu). It is the standard spelling Korean media use for the name. In Hangul it has three syllables: 주 + 디 + 스.

How do you pronounce Judith in Korean?

Say it ju-di-seu, with even weight on every syllable. 스 carries the filler vowel ㅡ (eu). Keep it short and light, almost just the consonant.

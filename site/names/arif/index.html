Arif in Korean 아리프

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Arif as 아리프: three syllables, said a-ri-peu. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Pronunciation tip

ㄹ between two vowels (아리) is one quick tap of the tongue, like the tt in American "butter": closer to a soft d than an English r.

  • 프 carries the filler vowel ㅡ (eu). Keep it short and light, almost just the consonant.
  • Give all 3 syllables the same weight. Korean has no strong stress, so it's a-ri-peu, evenly.

Korean given names are almost always two syllables (after a one-syllable family name), so at 3 syllables 아리프 reads as a foreign name at a glance. With foreign names some Koreans skip 야 and just say 아리프 or 아리프 씨.

Why it's spelled 아리프

Here the letters and the sound line up: applying the loanword rules letter by letter already gives 아리프, the same spelling Korean media use.

  • Korean has no f sound, so f becomes ㅍ (p), said with a puff of air.
  • Korean adds the vowel ㅡ to consonants that have no vowel of their own (프), which is why Arif has 3 syllables in Korean.

Questions people ask

How do you write Arif in Korean?

Arif is written 아리프 in Hangul (Revised Romanization: aripeu). It is the standard spelling Korean media use for the name. In Hangul it has three syllables: 아 + 리 + 프.

How do you pronounce Arif in Korean?

Say it a-ri-peu, with even weight on every syllable. ㄹ between two vowels (아리) is one quick tap of the tongue, like the tt in American "butter": closer to a soft d than an English r.

What does Arif mean in Korean?

On its own, nothing: 아리프 spells the sound of Arif, so the syllables are not Korean words and Koreans read it simply as a foreign name.

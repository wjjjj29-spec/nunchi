Pronunciation tip

The final ㅅ in 엇 is unreleased: stop the sound (t) without a puff of air. It is a t sound here, not s.

  • ㅓ (eo) in 엇 is the open "uh" of "sun", not an "o".
  • ㅘ in 와 is one gliding vowel: wa, in a single beat.
  • ㅇ at the top of 이 is silent, so 이 is just the vowel: i.
  • Give all 3 syllables the same weight. Korean has no strong stress, so it's wa-i-eot, evenly.

Korean given names are almost always two syllables (after a one-syllable family name), so at 3 syllables 와이엇 reads as a foreign name at a glance. In casual sentences Koreans often add 이 after a name that ends in a consonant: 와이엇이 왔어 ("Wyatt's here").

Spelled letter-by-letter you'd get 우얏, but Korean media write 와이엇, because the name is written the way it sounds, not the way it's spelled.

  • A final t is written with ㅅ under the syllable. It's still said as a held t.
  • The unstressed "uh" vowel is written ㅓ (eo), whatever letter English uses for it.
  • w merges with the vowel after it into one Korean vowel: 와.
  • Double letters count once. Hangul spells the sound, not the letters.

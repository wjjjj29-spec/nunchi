Booking the KTX as a foreigner: sites, fares and the Korail Pass

Tickets now open two months ahead, the SRT has merged into KTX and Seoul–Busan costs less. Here's how to book in 2026.

4 min readChecked 20 October 2026
At a glance1 / 6
The short version
  • Where to book: Korail's English site (letskorail.com now redirects to korail.com) or Klook, with your passport and a foreign card. No Korean number needed.
  • When: since 1 October 2026, tickets open two months before departure. Friday and Sunday trains sell out first.
  • Seoul to Busan: about 2h15m–2h45m, ₩54,400 standard since the September 2026 fare cut. SRT trains from Suseo are now sold as KTX.
  • Korail Pass: from ₩131,000 for 2 days. A Seoul–Busan return is cheaper, so it only pays off with three or more long rides.

Where to book

Use Korail's own website in English (korail.com; the old Let's Korail address, letskorail.com, now redirects there) or Klook, which has sold Korail tickets since April 2026. You sign up with an email address and your passport, with no Korean phone or ID number needed. The English site takes overseas Visa, Mastercard and UnionPay cards.

The app most Koreans use, Korail Talk, was renamed Korail+ in August 2026. Ticket counters accept overseas cards, and Seoul Station has a travel centre for foreign visitors with translation tablets.

Tickets now open two months ahead

Since 1 October 2026, train tickets go on sale two months before departure instead of one. Many guides still say 30 days. Weekday trains rarely sell out, but Friday evening and Sunday afternoon trains between Seoul and Busan do, so book once your dates are fixed. Emptier trains sometimes carry online special fares, 10–30% off.

KTX and SRT are now one network

Korail took over SR, the company that ran the SRT, on 1 September 2026. Trains from Suseo in southern Seoul now run as KTX (the former SRT trains are being renamed KTX-Sancheon) and are sold on the same Korail site and app, from one pool of seats. There's no separate SRT account to make, and checking SRT when KTX shows sold out no longer helps. Fares fell by about 10% on average to match old SRT prices.

  • Seoul Station: Busan, Daegu, Daejeon and Gangneung.
  • Yongsan: the southwest, such as Gwangju, Mokpo and Yeosu.
  • Suseo: handy if you're staying in Gangnam or Jamsil. Trains run south to Busan and the southwest.

Seoul to Busan: time, fare and seats

RouteTimeStandard fare
Seoul–Busanabout 2h15m–2h45m₩54,400

The standard fare was ₩59,800 until the September 2026 cut, and that older number is still all over the internet. Journey time depends on how many stops the train makes.

Standard class (일반실 ilbansil, standard room) has 2+2 seats with decent legroom. First class (특실 teuksil, special room) is 2+1 with wider seats and costs noticeably more. For a 2½-hour ride, standard is fine.

Sold out? Standing and free seats

When a train is full you can still buy a standing ticket (입석 ipseok, standing) at a small discount, and sit in any empty seat until its holder gets on. Some weekday trains also have unreserved cars (자유석 jayuseok, free seats): slightly cheaper, first come, first seated. Neither is fun with a suitcase, so book ahead when you can.

Korail Pass: is it worth it?

The Korail Pass (also sold as KR Pass) gives unlimited rides on KTX and most Korail trains on any 2, 3, 4 or 5 days within 10 days. It's for foreign visitors only, in your passport name.

Prices below are what the main resellers list in October 2026; Korail sets them, so check the final price when you buy.

Do the maths first. A Seoul–Busan return now costs ₩108,800, less than a 2-day pass, and the fare cut made the pass worse value. It only pays off with three or more long rides in a few days. You can reserve up to two seats a day; after that you ride standing. Sellers say pass seat reservations aren't available during public holiday periods. The pass doesn't cover AREX or the subway, and sellers still list SRT as excluded, so check whether a train from Suseo is covered before you reserve it. Chuseok and Seollal

The two big holidays don't follow the normal sale. Korail holds a special online-only sale for registered members a few weeks ahead, split by line, and the best trains go within minutes. In 2026 the Seollal sale opened in mid-January for mid-February travel, and the Chuseok sale ran in early September for late-September travel. Seollal 2027 falls in early February, so watch for the sale in January. Check dates on our Korean public holidays page. Missed out? Unpaid and cancelled seats come back in the days before, and intercity buses are the backup.

Boarding, luggage and e-tickets

  • No ticket gates. Your e-ticket on your phone is your ticket. Check the departure board for the platform, walk down and find your car number (호차 hocha, car) marked on the platform. Staff can check tickets on board, so keep it handy.
  • Stops are short. Be by the door with your bags before your station.
  • Luggage: overhead racks fit carry-ons. Big suitcases go in the space at the end of the car, which fills up on busy trains, so board early. Need to drop bags first? See luggage storage in Seoul.
  • Batteries: since 1 July 2026, Korail has banned high-capacity lithium batteries on its trains, reported as over 160Wh. Everyday power banks are well under that.
Nunchi tip

Arriving at Incheon and heading straight to Busan? Take the AREX to Seoul Station and book a KTX at least an hour later, to allow for immigration queues and the transfer.

Questions people ask

Can foreigners book KTX tickets online?

Yes. Korail's English website and Klook both take a passport and an overseas card, with no Korean phone number. The old Let's Korail address now redirects to korail.com.

How far in advance can I book KTX tickets?

Since 1 October 2026, two months before departure, up from one month. Chuseok and Seollal trains are sold in a separate holiday sale a few weeks before.

How much is the KTX from Seoul to Busan?

₩54,400 one way in standard class since September 2026, down from ₩59,800. The trip takes about 2 hours 15 minutes to 2 hours 45 minutes.

Is the Korail Pass worth it?

Only for three or more long rides in a few days. A 2-day adult pass is ₩131,000, while a Seoul–Busan return now costs ₩108,800.

What happened to the SRT?

Korail took over the SRT on 1 September 2026. Trains from Suseo now run as KTX and are booked on the same Korail website and app.

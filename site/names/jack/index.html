Jack in Korean 잭

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Jack as 잭: one syllable, said jaek. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Jack in Hangul
잭
Revised Romanization: jaek · said jaek · one syllable

Jack letter by letter

Each Hangul block is one syllable, built from a consonant, a vowel and sometimes a final consonant underneath.

잭
ㅈ j + ㅐ ae + ㄱ k (final)
jaek
Pronunciation tip

The final ㄱ in 잭 is unreleased: stop the sound (k) without a puff of air.

  • ㅐ (ae) is said like the e in "bed" by most Koreans today, a little more closed than the a in "cat".
  • ㅈ at the start is soft and unvoiced, halfway between ch and j.
  • It's a single syllable in Korean, so say it in one short beat: jaek.

How Koreans would call you

Friends calling you over
잭아!
잭 ends in a consonant, so it takes 아, said 재가. Casual, for close friends.
At work or with new people
잭 씨
잭 ssi. Polite, roughly "Mr" or "Ms", but used with the first name.
Customer, client, online
잭 님
잭 nim. What you'll see on a delivery box or a booking.

One syllable is short for Korea, where given names are almost always two, so 잭 stands out and is easy to remember. In casual sentences Koreans often add 이 after a name that ends in a consonant: 잭이 왔어 ("Jack's here").

Why it's spelled 잭

Here the letters and the sound line up: applying the loanword rules letter by letter already gives 잭, the same spelling Korean media use.

  • After a short vowel the final k tucks under the syllable as ㄱ, instead of getting its own syllable.

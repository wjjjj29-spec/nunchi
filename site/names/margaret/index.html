Margaret in Korean 마거릿

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Margaret as 마거릿: three syllables, said ma-geo-rit. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Pronunciation tip

ㄹ between two vowels (거릿) is one quick tap of the tongue, like the tt in American "butter": closer to a soft d than an English r.

  • The final ㅅ in 릿 is unreleased: stop the sound (t) without a puff of air. It is a t sound here, not s.
  • ㅓ (eo) in 거 is the open "uh" of "sun", not an "o".
  • Give all 3 syllables the same weight. Korean has no strong stress, so it's ma-geo-rit, evenly.

Korean given names are almost always two syllables (after a one-syllable family name), so at 3 syllables 마거릿 reads as a foreign name at a glance. In casual sentences Koreans often add 이 after a name that ends in a consonant: 마거릿이 왔어 ("Margaret's here").

How Koreans write it

Spelled letter-by-letter you'd get 마가렛, but Korean media write 마거릿, because the name is written the way it sounds, not the way it's spelled.

  • An r after a vowel (before a consonant or at the end) isn't written. Korean spelling follows British-style pronunciation, where that r is silent.
  • A final t is written with ㅅ under the syllable. It's still said as a held t.
  • The unstressed "uh" vowel is written ㅓ (eo), whatever letter English uses for it.

How do you pronounce Margaret in Korean?

Say it ma-geo-rit, with even weight on every syllable. ㄹ between two vowels (거릿) is one quick tap of the tongue, like the tt in American "butter": closer to a soft d than an English r.

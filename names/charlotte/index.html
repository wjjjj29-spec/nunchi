Charlotte in Korean 샬럿

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Charlotte as 샬럿: two syllables, said syal-leot. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Each Hangul block is one syllable, built from a consonant, a vowel and sometimes a final consonant underneath.

Pronunciation tip

The double ㄹ in 샬럿 is a clear English l, as in "hello", not an r.

  • The final ㅅ in 럿 is unreleased: stop the sound (t) without a puff of air. It is a t sound here, not s.
  • ㅓ (eo) in 럿 is the open "uh" of "sun", not an "o".
  • Two even beats, syal-leot: Korean doesn't stress one syllable over the other the way English does.

Why it's spelled 샬럿

Spelled letter-by-letter you'd get 찰로테, but Korean media write 샬럿, because the name is written the way it sounds, not the way it's spelled.

  • An r after a vowel (before a consonant or at the end) isn't written. Korean spelling follows British-style pronunciation, where that r is silent.
  • An l between vowels is written twice (final ㄹ + ㄹ), so Koreans say a clear l instead of a tapped r.
  • The unstressed "uh" vowel is written ㅓ (eo), whatever letter English uses for it.
  • Double letters count once. Hangul spells the sound, not the letters.

How do you write Charlotte in Korean?

How do you pronounce Charlotte in Korean?

Say it syal-leot, with even weight on every syllable. The double ㄹ in 샬럿 is a clear English l, as in "hello", not an r.

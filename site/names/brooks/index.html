Brooks in Korean 브룩스

Korean media, baristas and the immigration office write Brooks as 브룩스: three syllables, said beu-ruk-seu. Here's how it's built, how to say it, and what people will call you.

Pronunciation tip

ㄹ between two vowels (브룩) is one quick tap of the tongue, like the tt in American "butter": closer to a soft d than an English r.

  • 브 and 스 carry the filler vowel ㅡ (eu). Keep them short and light, almost just the consonant.
  • The final ㄱ in 룩 is unreleased: stop the sound (k) without a puff of air.
  • ㅂ at the start is soft and unvoiced, halfway between p and b.
  • Give all 3 syllables the same weight. Korean has no strong stress, so it's beu-ruk-seu, evenly.

How Koreans would call you

Friends calling you over
브룩스야!
브룩스 ends in a vowel, so it takes 야. Casual, for close friends.
At work or with new people
브룩스 씨
브룩스 ssi. Polite, roughly "Mr" or "Ms", but used with the first name.
Customer, client, online
브룩스 님
브룩스 nim. What you'll see on a delivery box or a booking.

Korean given names are almost always two syllables (after a one-syllable family name), so at 3 syllables 브룩스 reads as a foreign name at a glance. With foreign names some Koreans skip 야 and just say 브룩스 or 브룩스 씨.

Spelled letter-by-letter you'd get 브루크스, but Korean media write 브룩스, because the name is written the way it sounds, not the way it's spelled.

  • Korean adds the vowel ㅡ to consonants that have no vowel of their own (브 and 스), which is why Brooks has 3 syllables in Korean.
